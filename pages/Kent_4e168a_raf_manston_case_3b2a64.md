## Introduction

The RAF Manston UFO interception mystery is one of Kent’s most famous Cold War-era aerial incidents because it combined three elements that normally give a sighting unusual weight: military radar, a trained fighter pilot and an armed interception order. On 20 May 1957, USAF Lieutenant Milton Torres was reportedly scrambled from RAF Manston near Ramsgate in an F-86D Sabre after an unidentified radar contact appeared over eastern England. The encounter became famous because Torres later said he gained radar lock on a target, was ordered to fire 24 rockets, and saw the contact disappear before he could attack.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2008-10-21/us-pilot-was-ordered-to-shoot-down-ufo/548314" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News US pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUS pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO - ABC NewsOctober 21, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 21, 2008</span></span></span>

The case remains unresolved, but that does not mean the evidence proves an extraordinary craft. ## Radar contact and fighter response

RAF Manston’s role in the incident was central. In the 1950s the station was a strategically important airfield used by the United States Air Force, and fighter-interceptor units operated there as part of the wider Cold War defence network. The reported incident involved F-86D Sabre interceptors from a USAF unit on alert duty at Manston.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.manstonhistory.org.uk/usaf-f-86d-manston-ordered-fire-ufo-may-20th-1957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: manstonhistory.org.uk">[History of Manston Airfield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">manstonhistory.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History of Manston Airfield USAF F-86D from Manston ordered to fire on UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History of Manston AirfieldUSAF F-86D from Manston ordered to fire on UFO - May 20th 1957 - History of Manston AirfieldMay 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2025</span></span></span>

According to Torres’s later account, a ground controller directed him towards an unidentified radar target detected over eastern England. He reported climbing to intercept the object and obtaining a radar return from his aircraft’s own system. The target was described as producing an unusually large radar image, comparable in size to a large bomber, and Torres later recalled it as having the appearance of a “flying aircraft carrier”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2008-10-21/us-pilot-was-ordered-to-shoot-down-ufo/548314" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News US pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUS pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO - ABC NewsOctober 21, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 21, 2008</span></span></span>

The most striking part of the story is the reported weapons order. Torres said he was instructed to fire a full salvo of rockets at the target after receiving authorisation from the controller. Before he could launch, he said the radar contact disappeared and the interception ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2008-10-21/us-pilot-was-ordered-to-shoot-down-ufo/548314" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News US pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUS pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO - ABC NewsOctober 21, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 21, 2008</span></span></span>

This sequence made the Manston case stand apart from many UFO reports. It was not simply a distant light seen by a member of the public; it involved a military response system designed to identify and react to possible threats. However, the same factor also introduces caution. Defence radar operations are complex environments where unidentified returns, equipment limitations and communication errors can have serious consequences, especially during periods of heightened alert.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National ArchivesMay 23, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2019</span></span></span>

A major weakness is the lack of a complete surviving technical record. Later public discussion has relied heavily on Torres’s recollection and released Ministry of Defence UFO files rather than a full set of radar tapes, operational logs or contemporaneous fighter records. The absence of these records makes it difficult to independently reconstruct exactly what happened minute by minute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.manstonhistory.org.uk/usaf-f-86d-manston-ordered-fire-ufo-may-20th-1957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: manstonhistory.org.uk">[History of Manston Airfield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">manstonhistory.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History of Manston Airfield USAF F-86D from Manston ordered to fire on UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History of Manston AirfieldUSAF F-86D from Manston ordered to fire on UFO - May 20th 1957 - History of Manston AirfieldMay 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2025</span></span></span>

## Cold War defence context

The Manston incident happened during a period when unidentified radar contacts were treated as potential security issues rather than simply unusual sightings. The Cold War created genuine concern about unidentified aircraft because a radar contact could represent hostile reconnaissance, new technology, or a false indication from imperfect systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National ArchivesMay 23, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2019</span></span></span>

British air defence during the 1950s depended on a network of radar stations and interception procedures that were still evolving. The National Archives has highlighted that radar technology of the era had limitations, including problems caused by interference, reflections and changing system capabilities. These issues were important when assessing unusual reports from the period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National ArchivesMay 23, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2019</span></span></span>

This context matters because the Manston case is sometimes presented as evidence that military authorities encountered something beyond known technology. A more cautious interpretation is that the event occurred in an environment where unusual radar returns were expected to be investigated seriously. An unidentified contact was not automatically an unknown machine; it was a problem requiring identification.

The later release of Ministry of Defence UFO files in the 2000s renewed interest in the case. Reports from the file releases brought Torres’s account to a wider audience and showed why the incident had remained one of the better-known UK military UFO stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/p/22xqp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span> However, the release of documents did not provide a definitive explanation.

## Possible explanations and remaining doubts

Several explanations have been proposed for the Manston interception, with varying levels of evidence.

**A radar or tracking error** is one possibility. Radar systems of the period were powerful but not infallible, and unusual returns could be produced by technical problems, atmospheric effects or interference. The difficulty for this explanation is that the reported event involved both ground radar and an airborne interceptor radar contact, which supporters of the mystery argue makes a simple malfunction less convincing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National ArchivesMay 23, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2019</span></span></span>

**Electronic warfare or radar deception** has also been suggested. Military forces were developing methods of confusing enemy radar during the Cold War, and later researchers have discussed whether the incident could have involved a deliberately created false radar target. The idea is attractive because it fits the era’s intelligence environment, but there is no publicly available proof that the Manston event was such a test.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uapmedia.uk/articles/uapradar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmedia.uk">[UAP Media UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmedia.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Media UKUAP on RADAR: Some Common Explanations — UAP Media UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Media UKUAP on RADAR: Some Common Explanations — UAP Media UK</span></span></span>

Some writers have linked the case with later-known electronic warfare projects, including the American radar deception programme known as Palladium. However, using later programmes to explain a 1957 event remains speculative, and a direct connection has not been demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uapmedia.uk/articles/uapradar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmedia.uk">[UAP Media UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmedia.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Media UKUAP on RADAR: Some Common Explanations — UAP Media UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Media UKUAP on RADAR: Some Common Explanations — UAP Media UK</span></span></span>

**A conventional aircraft or other physical object** is another possibility, although the available public evidence does not identify a specific candidate. The lack of a visual sighting is significant: Torres reported tracking the object on radar but did not see it with his own eyes. This separates the case from pilot sightings where an unusual object’s shape, movement or appearance can be independently assessed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2008-10-21/us-pilot-was-ordered-to-shoot-down-ufo/548314" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News US pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUS pilot was ordered to shoot down UFO - ABC NewsOctober 21, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 21, 2008</span></span></span>

The “alien spacecraft” interpretation remains the most dramatic version of the story, but it relies on assumptions beyond the available evidence. The documented facts show an unusual radar and interception episode; they do not demonstrate the origin or nature of the target.

## Why the Manston case still matters in Kent’s UFO history


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The RAF Manston interception remains important because it represents the strongest type of UFO claim: one involving professional observers, military equipment and an official response. At the same time, it also demonstrates why even serious reports can remain uncertain. A radar contact can be real without its cause being understood, and an unresolved case is not automatically evidence for an extraordinary explanation.

</div>

Within Kent’s wider UFO history, Manston is therefore best viewed as a Cold War aviation mystery rather than a confirmed unexplained craft encounter. Its lasting appeal comes from the unanswered questions: what did the radar operators see, what did Torres lock onto, why was an armed response considered necessary, and why has no complete explanation emerged? The surviving evidence supports the conclusion that a significant defence incident occurred, while leaving the identity of the target open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.manstonhistory.org.uk/usaf-f-86d-manston-ordered-fire-ufo-may-20th-1957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: manstonhistory.org.uk">[History of Manston Airfield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">manstonhistory.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History of Manston Airfield USAF F-86D from Manston ordered to fire on UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History of Manston AirfieldUSAF F-86D from Manston ordered to fire on UFO - May 20th 1957 - History of Manston AirfieldMay 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2025</span></span></span>
