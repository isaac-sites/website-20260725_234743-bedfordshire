## Introduction

A [Tyrone]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-tyrones-ufo/' | relative_url }}) UFO map is not simply a matter of placing dots inside today’s council boundaries. The project’s Tyrone is the historic county: a geographical area extending from the Strabane and Sperrin country in the west to the Dungannon–Cookstown district and Lough Neagh in the east. Modern public bodies divide that same ground among several councils, police districts, constituencies and archive descriptions. A report made near Fivemiletown, Strabane or the River Blackwater may therefore be indexed under a modern authority, a neighbouring county, “Northern Ireland”, or only the nearest town.

This matters because Tyrone’s known sightings are already thinly documented. A misplaced location can make one report appear to be several cases, remove it from a county search altogether, or create a false cluster around whichever town an editor selected as the dateline. The most reliable map is consequently layered rather than absolute: historic county lines for the project’s organising geography, modern administrative areas for finding records, and precise towns, roads, townlands and viewing directions wherever the original account supplies them.

## Historic Tyrone does not fit one modern district

Historic County Tyrone remains a useful cultural and geographical unit, but it ceased to be the basic structure for local government long ago. Since Northern Ireland’s council reorganisation, much of central and eastern Tyrone has fallen within Mid Ulster, while Omagh and a large western and south-western area sit within Fermanagh and Omagh. The Strabane district is administered with Derry, and some places close to the historic county’s eastern and southern edges are more readily encountered in records associated with neighbouring administrative areas. The result is that none of the three most relevant modern council names is synonymous with County Tyrone:

* **Mid Ulster** includes many unmistakably Tyrone places, including Dungannon, Cookstown, Coalisland, Pomeroy, Clogher, Fivemiletown, Aughnacloy and Killyman, but it also extends into historic County Londonderry.
* **Fermanagh and Omagh** contains Omagh, Fintona, Dromore, Drumquin, Gortin, Sixmilecross and Trillick, alongside the whole of modern County Fermanagh.
* **Derry City and Strabane** contains Strabane, Sion Mills and the Glenelly valley, but its name naturally encourages catalogue users to think first of Derry rather than Tyrone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nidsr/2012/9780337987786/schedules" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legislation.gov.uk">[Legislation.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legislation.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Local Government (Boundaries) Order (Northern Ireland) 2012Banbridge and Craigavon Belfast Causeway Coast and Glens Derry and Strabane Fe...</span></span></span>

Police geography follows the councils. The Police Service of Northern Ireland operates 11 local policing districts that are coterminous with council boundaries. A modern police log from historic Tyrone might therefore be held or released under Mid Ulster, Fermanagh and Omagh, or Derry City and Strabane rather than under a single “Tyrone” heading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psni.police.uk/about-us/local-policing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psni.police.uk">[PSNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psni.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Service of Northern Ireland Roadsafe Roadshow Local Policing A visible, accessible a...</span></span></span>

This is not merely an administrative curiosity. It changes what a researcher finds. A search for “Tyrone UFO” may miss an Omagh report categorised by council, a Strabane incident labelled “Derry and Strabane”, or a Dungannon-area call listed only as “Mid Ulster”. Conversely, a search for “Mid Ulster sightings” will bring in locations outside historic Tyrone. Counts assembled without checking the actual place can therefore either understate or overstate the county’s record.

## Border sightings need more than a town name

Tyrone touches more county boundaries than a simple modern map may suggest. Its historic neighbours are Donegal to the west and north-west, Londonderry to the north-east, Armagh to the east and south-east, Monaghan to the south, and Fermanagh to the south-west; Lough Neagh forms much of its eastern edge. The River Blackwater also marks part of the historic boundary towards Monaghan and Armagh.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nir-roots.org/tyrone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nir-roots.org">[nir-roots.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nir-roots.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NIR Roots County Tyrone, Northern Ireland Gen Web&gt; #### County Tyrone Geography</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">&gt; THE county of Tyrone is inland, being bounded by the county of Donegal on the north, and north- weft; by the county of Londonderry, on...</span></span></span>

Several places associated with Tyrone sighting reports lie in precisely the areas where loose location labels cause trouble. Fivemiletown and Clogher sit near the Fermanagh and Monaghan approaches. Aughnacloy and Caledon are close to the international border and County Armagh. Dungannon, Killyman and Moy lie near the Blackwater corridor. Strabane faces Donegal across the River Foyle and is now routinely presented through a cross-county council name.

A witness usually reports from lived geography, not cartographic geography. Someone driving near Fivemiletown may describe an object as being “over Fermanagh”, “towards Clogher” or “on the Monaghan side” without knowing its position. A light observed above a distant ridge might be several miles beyond the observer’s county. Where a report gives only the witness’s location, plotting the phenomenon at that same point is misleading.

The Killyman–Dungannon account illustrates the problem. In a report submitted to the US-based National UFO Reporting Center, a witness described a large orange object approaching from the direction of Armagh, stopping near Killyman and later changing direction around Newmills and Dungannon. Even if the narrative were accepted exactly as written, it describes a route across a viewing area rather than one fixed point. Its date was approximate and the submission was made years after the alleged event, so a line on a map would imply more positional precision than the source warrants. The most honest representation would distinguish the observer’s stated locations, the claimed direction of approach and the uncertain path.

The much-repeated 2004 Fivemiletown “diamond-shaped craft” presents another caution. The claim appeared in a newspaper list attributed to the UFO Society of Ireland, but Fivemiletown does not appear in the Ministry of Defence’s published table of 2004 reports. That does not prove the civilian account was false; it shows that a report circulating through a UFO group and the press was not necessarily reported to, retained by or indexed by the MOD. Treating civilian and official lists as if they were one continuous dataset would conceal that distinction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/BritishUFOFiles/BritishMODUFOsightings2004.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveTable of all UFO sightings 2004Table of all UFO sightings 2004 BRITISH MOD SIGHTING REPORTS - 2004 DATE TIME TOWN / VILLA...</span></span></span>

Border geography also affects possible explanations. Tyrone’s principal roads connect Omagh with Ballygawley and Dungannon, Dungannon with Armagh and Belfast, and Strabane with Derry and Donegal. Reports from drivers frequently use roads, junctions and distant settlements as reference points. Aircraft, helicopters, vehicle reflections, telecommunications masts and bright astronomical objects can appear to move relative to a winding road or changing horizon. Without the witness’s direction of travel, compass bearing, elevation and duration, even an apparently detailed sequence may be impossible to reconstruct.

## Rural horizons can exaggerate apparent movement

Much of Tyrone offers long but uneven sightlines: open uplands in and around the Sperrins, broad agricultural country around Cookstown and Dungannon, drumlin landscapes towards Monaghan, and valleys broken by ridges, woodland and hedges. These conditions are good for noticing distant lights, but poor for judging their size and range.

A light seen just above a ridge may be interpreted as hovering over the nearest named village even when it lies far beyond it. When the observer is moving by car, foreground trees and bends in the road can make a stationary light appear to follow, stop or change direction. A bright planet low in the sky can remain visible for a long time while seeming to shift against hedges, roofs and telegraph poles. Aircraft approaching almost head-on may look stationary before banking and revealing coloured navigation lights.

These are possibilities rather than automatic explanations. They become important because many Tyrone reports lack the information needed to eliminate them. Descriptions such as “orange object”, “bright star-like light”, “silent”, “hovering” or “shot away” do not by themselves establish distance or speed. A map should not convert those impressions into a measured flight path.

The same restraint applies to apparent clusters. Repeated reports around Omagh or Dungannon may partly reflect population, road use and newspaper coverage rather than an unusual concentration in the sky. Larger towns supply more observers, more police contacts and clearer datelines. Rural witnesses may also name the nearest recognisable town even when they are several miles away. What appears as an urban cluster can therefore represent a dispersed viewing area gathered under one convenient label.

A useful Tyrone map should show confidence in the location as well as the location itself:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Point location:** a clearly identified street, townland, airfield or building.
* **Approximate locality:** a named town or village without a precise observation point.
* **Route report:** an event described from a moving vehicle or across several places.
* **Directional report:** the witness location is known, but the object was only said to be “towards” another place.
* **County-only report:** the source provides “Tyrone” or “Northern Ireland” with no recoverable locality.

</div>

Keeping those categories separate prevents a vague county-level entry from carrying the same visual authority as a report tied to a precise place and time.

## Archive searches must follow several geographies

The Public Record Office of Northern Ireland’s historical map viewer provides a practical way to reconcile these layers. It allows searches by townland, parish, town or modern postal address and displays county, parish and townland boundaries over historical Ordnance Survey mapping and modern basemaps. For reports recorded under a farm, crossroads or obscure rural locality, townland identification may be more useful than the council name.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.proni.gov.uk/proni-historical-maps-viewer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: proni.gov.uk">[proni.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">proni.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on proni.gov.uk.</span></span></span>

Townlands are especially important in Tyrone. PRONI provides a county-specific townland list, while the Townlands database records more than 2,000 townlands in County Tyrone, distributed across civil parishes, baronies and electoral divisions. The same locality may consequently appear in records under a townland, parish, barony, postal town or council district.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.proni.gov.uk/publications/townlands-county" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: proni.gov.uk">[proni.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">proni.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Townlands by County &#124; The Public Record Office of Northern Ireland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Townlands by County &#124; The Public Record Office of Northern Ireland</span></span></span>

For a serious sighting search, the most productive approach is to work outwards from the place rather than relying on “Tyrone” as a keyword. A Clogher investigation, for example, should test combinations including Clogher, Augher, Fivemiletown, Ballygawley, Mid Ulster, Omagh, Fermanagh, Monaghan and Northern Ireland, then examine dates and descriptions for duplicates. A Strabane search should include Strabane, Sion Mills, Derry and Strabane, County Tyrone, County Londonderry, Donegal and the Foyle valley.

Official UFO records create an additional layer. The Ministry of Defence’s published tables normally give a date, time, town or village, broad county or region and a brief description. The National Archives notes that surviving material includes sighting reports, correspondence and policy files, but records before the 1960s were often destroyed after five years. Consequently, absence from the surviving MOD files is not proof that no report was made, particularly for older decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O reports in the UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF O reports in the UK</span></span></span>

MOD tables may also use “Northern Ireland” as the county field rather than one of the six historic counties. In the published 2003 table, for example, Belfast entries are categorised broadly under Northern Ireland. That filing style makes a full-text place-name search essential; filtering only by a county column would not recover Tyrone systematically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75c656e5274a545822e1ea/UFOReports2003WholeoftheUK.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on service.gov.uk.</span></span></span>

Police information has similar limitations. A recent PSNI disclosure of logs containing terms such as “UFO”, “UAP”, “alien” and “spaceship” demonstrates that calls are recorded under the caller’s reported town or general location. The released examples are brief incident-log summaries rather than investigated case files, and the search depends on particular words having been entered by call handlers. A strange-light report recorded as a suspected drone, aircraft or flare could therefore be absent from a “UFO” keyword return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psni.police.uk/foi-disclosure-log/sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psni.police.uk">[PSNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psni.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on police.uk.</span></span></span>

Regional broadcasting and newspapers add another route into the record. PRONI holds the UTV archive in partnership with Northern Ireland Screen and ITV, but much of it has not been digitised, while access to some material is restricted or requires a specific request. Historic newspaper databases likewise show that Northern Ireland UFO stories were commonly written as province-wide episodes, sometimes with several locations compressed into one report. A Tyrone witness may be buried in an article headed “Ulster UFO mystery” rather than indexed by county.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.proni.gov.uk/accessing-utv-archive" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: proni.gov.uk">[proni.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">proni.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on proni.gov.uk.</span></span></span>

## What a trustworthy Tyrone sightings map should show

The best map for this project should keep the historic county as its centre of gravity without pretending that every source used the same geography. Each plotted record should retain at least four distinct fields: the source’s original location wording, the interpreted historic county, the relevant modern council or police district, and the confidence level of the placement.

Where possible, the map should also separate the **observer’s position** from the **claimed position of the object**. A report made from Killyman about a light approaching from Armagh is not simply a Killyman point. A sighting from a road between Omagh and Ballygawley may deserve a route segment rather than a town marker. An account saying “over Fivemiletown” without a witness address should be plotted as an approximate locality, not attached to the village centre with false precision.

Duplicate control is equally important. Repetition across a UFO database, magazine, newspaper story and later compilation does not necessarily represent four witnesses. Matching date, wording, shape, direction and place often reveals that several entries descend from one original submission. The map should link such versions under one case record while preserving their different publication histories.

Finally, cross-border observations should not be forced into Tyrone merely because the observer stood within the county. The Republic of Ireland’s counties are geographical context on the project map, not UK branches. A light seen from Aughnacloy towards Monaghan may be relevant to Tyrone’s sighting history because that is where the witness was located, but the map should not silently classify the claimed aerial position as part of the UK collection.

The resulting picture is less dramatic than a conventional “UFO hotspot” map, but more useful. Tyrone’s reports do not currently form a securely documented concentration with precise flight paths or independently verified boundaries. They form a scattered body of testimony whose apparent pattern changes according to whether records are searched by historic county, council, police district, townland, road, nearest town or national jurisdiction. Mapping those differences is not a technical footnote; it is central to deciding whether the county contains a genuine local pattern or merely a set of loosely located reports gathered under changing names.

    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
