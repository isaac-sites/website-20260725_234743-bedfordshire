## Introduction

The Christmas Eve 2020 Maghera sighting is best understood as an **unresolved but weakly evidenced night-light report**, not as proof of an extraordinary craft. Two witnesses described ten red-and-white lights arriving one after another, accompanied by a slight electric-sounding hum. The lights approached from north of Maghera, turned south towards Tobermore and disappeared into thin cloud or fog. That directional detail makes the account more useful than many local UFO reports, but there is no photograph, video, radar record or independent witness statement with which to test it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.niufos.org/post/ufo-report-ufo-sighting-county-londonderry-maghera-24th-december-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: niufos.org">[NIUFOS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">niufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report ufo sighting county londonderry maghera 24th december 2020</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report &#124; UFO Sighting &#124; County Londonderry &#124; Maghera &#124; 24th December 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: december 2020</span></span></span>

Aircraft remain a plausible explanation, while several small drones could reproduce the reported hum, regular spacing and apparent sharp turn particularly well. ## The Christmas Eve 2020 flight path

The report concerns an evening walk on Tullyheran Road in Maghera at approximately 6.15 pm on 24 December 2020. The witnesses described calm, cold and dry conditions with thin cloud. Their observation lasted an estimated eight or nine minutes, long enough for them to watch a sequence rather than a momentary flash.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.niufos.org/post/ufo-report-ufo-sighting-county-londonderry-maghera-24th-december-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: niufos.org">[NIUFOS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">niufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report ufo sighting county londonderry maghera 24th december 2020</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report &#124; UFO Sighting &#124; County Londonderry &#124; Maghera &#124; 24th December 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: december 2020</span></span></span>

A second object followed the same route before the first disappeared, followed by further lights until ten had passed at apparently uniform intervals. Tobermore lies about two and a half miles south-south-west of Maghera, so the reported turn was geographically coherent rather than a vague statement that the objects simply “flew away”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.niufos.org/post/ufo-report-ufo-sighting-county-londonderry-maghera-24th-december-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: niufos.org">[NIUFOS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">niufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report ufo sighting county londonderry maghera 24th december 2020</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report &#124; UFO Sighting &#124; County Londonderry &#124; Maghera &#124; 24th December 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: december 2020</span></span></span>

## What red lights and humming sounds can indicate

A steady or slowly changing red point is not an unusual aviation feature. Aircraft flying at night use navigation lights to indicate orientation and anti-collision lights to attract attention. Depending on viewing angle, atmospheric haze and the brightness of individual lamps, an observer may see one colour dominate while other lights are hidden by the airframe, lost in glare or too faint to distinguish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/965-2012/Content/Document%20Structure/08%20SPO/2%20Regs/24790_SPOIDEA115_Operating_lights.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk">[Regulatory Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Regulatory LibrarySPO.IDE.A.115 Operating lightsSPO.IDE.A.115 Operating lights You are here: Annex VIII (Part-SPO) &gt; SUBPART D: INSTRUMEN...</span></span></span>

This matters because the Maghera witnesses did not report seeing the outline, wings or body of any object. They saw red-and-white lighting and inferred motion, height and turning from those lights. At night, a luminous point supplies poor distance information: a nearby small object and a distant larger aircraft can occupy a similar apparent size in the sky. Without distance, neither speed nor altitude can be calculated reliably.

The comparison with “landing lights” is also revealing. Landing lights can look exceptionally bright when an aircraft is approaching roughly towards an observer, then weaken or disappear quickly when it changes heading. A turn towards Tobermore could therefore make an approaching light appear to vanish into mist even if the aircraft itself continued normally. Thin cloud can intensify this effect by spreading the glow when the lamp points towards the observer and obscuring it after the angle changes.

The ten-object sequence is harder to fit to one aircraft but not necessarily to normal aviation as a whole. Aircraft following a shared route can appear evenly spaced, especially when seen almost head-on. What appears to be a sharp turn may instead be several aircraft independently reaching the same point along a corridor or circuit before changing course. Against that interpretation, the witnesses said the lights were unusually similar, relatively low-looking and accompanied by the same hum. No timetable or complete air-traffic record has been produced to test whether ten aircraft could have occupied the relevant sightline.

The sound offers a clue but not a verdict. A conventional aeroplane at the claimed altitude would not usually be described as producing a slight electric-motor hum, although distance, wind and terrain can alter what reaches an observer. A helicopter is capable of turning and following a local route, but ten similarly spaced helicopters would be a conspicuous movement and would normally produce more substantial rotor noise.

Small multicopter drones naturally generate a sustained buzzing or humming tone from their electric motors and rapidly rotating propellers. Technical work on unmanned-aircraft acoustics identifies propeller and rotor noise as a defining part of their sound, affected by configuration, manoeuvring and propagation through the surrounding environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sto.nato.int/document/assessment-and-reduction-of-installed-propeller-and-rotor-noise-from-unmanned-aircraft/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sto.nato.int">[NATO STO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sto.nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATO STOAssessment and Reduction of Installed Propeller and Rotor Noise from Unmanned Aircraft &#124; NATO Science and Technology Organization...</span></span></span>

A drone explanation fits several reported features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It can travel slowly enough to be watched for several minutes.
* It can alter direction sharply without banking through a wide aeroplane-like turn.
* Its electric propulsion can produce the reported slight hum.
* Its small body may be invisible while its LEDs remain conspicuous.
* Several drones can follow the same programmed route at regular intervals.

</div>

The last point is important. The account cannot sensibly be explained as one drone seen ten times unless it repeatedly circled out of sight, which the witnesses did not describe. A drone hypothesis requires **multiple drones**, or repeated launches from roughly the same location. Organised recreational flights, testing or coordinated filming could produce such a sequence, but no operator or local event has been linked to it.

Red illumination is compatible with consumer drones without being diagnostic. Drone lighting varies by manufacturer and model, and may include red, green, white or status-indicator LEDs. Some systems use different colours to show orientation, connection state or aircraft condition. Consequently, “red light” no longer separates an ordinary aircraft from an unmanned one; it merely establishes that the object carried visible lighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dl.djicdn.com/downloads/DJI_Mavic_3/DJI_Mavic_3_User_Manual_v1.0_enI.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dl.djicdn.com">[DJI Downloads]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dl.djicdn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DJI Downloads User Manual</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DJI Downloads User Manual</span></span></span>

## How drones changed modern UFO identification

Older UFO assessments often treated abrupt stops, hovering and tight directional changes as evidence against conventional aircraft. That reasoning is much less persuasive for sightings made after camera drones became widely available. A multicopter does not need forward airspeed to remain airborne. By independently changing the speed of its rotors, it can hover, rotate on the spot, reverse and move sideways.

This makes the Maghera turn towards Tobermore less anomalous than it might first sound. Aeroplanes usually change heading in a smooth arc, but a drone can pivot rapidly. Aviation specialists discussing modern night sightings have emphasised this distinction: planes and helicopters generally move smoothly, whereas multicopters can stop abruptly, make tight turns and reverse course. The same specialists caution that a distant light by itself rarely allows a confident classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/57a0d051a2a94d21787089ceaf47b175" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News Is that a drone or a plane? Experts help explain the differences</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AP News Is that a drone or a plane? Experts help explain the differences</span></span></span>

Drones also complicate the use of sound. A faint mechanical or electrical buzz can support a nearby multicopter interpretation, but it does not establish that the sound and light shared the same source. At night, an observer may unconsciously combine a distant visual target with a nearer road vehicle, electrical installation or unseen aircraft. In the Maghera case, the reported recurrence of the hum with successive lights makes that coincidence less attractive, although the testimony was written after the event rather than captured in a time-stamped recording.

Modern drone rules demonstrate how seriously lighting and visibility are now treated. Current UK Civil Aviation Authority guidance requires Open Category drones flown at night to carry an active green flashing light, intended partly to distinguish them from crewed aircraft. That requirement took effect in 2026 and therefore cannot be projected backwards onto the 2020 Maghera incident. It does, however, underline the underlying identification problem: regulators recognise that small illuminated aircraft at night can otherwise be difficult to classify from the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/getting-started-with-drones-and-model-aircraft/flying-at-night-in-the-open-category/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caa.co.uk">[CAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caa.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on caa.co.uk.</span></span></span>

A robust investigation of a similar sighting today would need more than a witness description. Investigators would seek original video with surrounding stars or landmarks, compass bearings, angular elevation, exact timings for each object, weather and cloud-base information, complete air-traffic data, and enquiries about local drone operations. A recording of the sound could also help distinguish the higher-frequency buzz of small rotors from a piston engine, turbine or helicopter.

None of that material survives publicly for Maghera. The reported altitude cannot be trusted because the lights had no known size. The sharp turn was not filmed. The regular spacing was not timed. Public flight tracking excluded one obvious class of candidate but did not establish that the airspace contained no conventional aircraft. The drone theory is mechanically convincing, yet remains unverified because no launch point, operator or drone formation was found.

## Was the Maghera red light a drone or aircraft?

The most defensible answer is that **both remain plausible, with multiple drones offering the closest match to the combination of humming, repeated spacing and a tight change of direction**. Conventional aircraft better explain bright red-and-white aviation-style lighting and a shared route, but ten apparently identical movements and the quiet electric-like sound are less comfortable fits unless the witnesses were watching traffic at greater distance than they realised.

Chinese lanterns are a weaker alternative. A group released one after another could produce warm lights at regular intervals, and cloud could hide them. However, lanterns drift with the wind rather than repeatedly execute the same clear turn, and they would not normally create a detectable electric hum. The witnesses reported calm conditions, although local winds above ground level were not documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.niufos.org/post/ufo-report-ufo-sighting-county-londonderry-maghera-24th-december-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: niufos.org">[NIUFOS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">niufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report ufo sighting county londonderry maghera 24th december 2020</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report &#124; UFO Sighting &#124; County Londonderry &#124; Maghera &#124; 24th December 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: december 2020</span></span></span>

The case should therefore remain classified as **unresolved, but probably conventional**. “Unresolved” describes the limits of the surviving record; it does not mean that the lights displayed technology beyond known aircraft. Later knowledge has arguably weakened the extraordinary reading because drone behaviour provides a straightforward mechanism for the most unusual elements. Within County Londonderry’s wider UFO history, Maghera is valuable less as a mystery craft case than as a lesson in modern identification. Its direction details, duration and two witnesses make it stronger than an isolated claim of a light in the sky. Its missing imagery, uncertain altitude and incomplete traffic checks prevent a firm conclusion. The red glow and hum were genuinely unidentified to the observers, but the reported behaviour now sits comfortably within the capabilities of ordinary drones as well as some conventional aviation scenarios.
