## Introduction

The short cine film taken by Peter Day near Cuddington on 11 January 1973 almost certainly records a real and unusual-looking aerial event. The strongest later evidence, however, points not to an unknown craft but to burning fuel released from a damaged United States Air Force F-111 shortly after take-off from RAF Upper Heyford. Radio timings reportedly place the aircraft emergency, fuel dumping and sightings in the same few minutes and general area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Northern UFO News No 156</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Northern UFO News No 156</span></span></span>

The case remains important in [Buckinghamshire]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-buckinghamshires/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history because it shows how incomplete information can preserve a mystery. Early accounts linked Day’s orange light only loosely to an aircraft that crashed about 40 minutes later, making the timings appear incompatible. When more detailed accident and radio information emerged, the dispute changed substantially. The surviving film is still interesting evidence of what witnesses saw, but it cannot establish that the source was extraordinary.

## What Peter Day and the other witnesses reported

Peter Day was a building surveyor living at Moreton, just south of Thame. Shortly after 9am on 11 January 1973, he was driving east along the A418 towards Aylesbury when he noticed an orange or fiery light in the overcast sky. He stopped in a lay-by near Cuddington, on the Oxfordshire–Buckinghamshire side of the route, and used a cine camera kept in his car to record approximately 23 seconds of film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://strangethame.co.uk/is-aylesbury-vale-a-ufo-corridor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: strangethame.co.uk">[Strange Thame]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">strangethame.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Strange Thame Is Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Strange ThameIs Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor? - Strange Thame...</span></span></span>

Day reportedly watched the light for longer than the film itself lasted. Accounts describe it as a distinct orange ball rather than an obvious aircraft, with no clearly visible wings, fuselage or conventional navigation lights. Its disappearance was especially memorable: Day said that it did not appear to climb, descend or travel out of view, but was present in one film frame and absent in the next.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://strangethame.co.uk/is-aylesbury-vale-a-ufo-corridor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: strangethame.co.uk">[Strange Thame]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">strangethame.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Strange Thame Is Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Strange ThameIs Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor? - Strange Thame...</span></span></span>

The report was not based on Day’s observation alone. When he called at the *Thame Gazette*, he was told that children and a teacher at Chilton Primary School had also seen the phenomenon. These witnesses reportedly heard little or no aircraft noise, an absence later used to argue against a jet explanation. Contemporary police information nevertheless attributed the sighting to fuel released by a military aircraft in difficulty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://strangethame.co.uk/is-aylesbury-vale-a-ufo-corridor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: strangethame.co.uk">[Strange Thame]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">strangethame.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Strange Thame Is Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Strange ThameIs Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor? - Strange Thame...</span></span></span>

Several features made the case stronger than a routine light-in-the-sky report:

* Day stopped and filmed the phenomenon rather than relying solely on memory.
* Other observers were reported from a separate location.
* The observation occurred in daylight, although beneath an overcast sky.
* A genuine military aviation emergency was unfolding in the surrounding region.
* The event was reported locally rather than emerging only through much later UFO literature.

Those strengths concern the sincerity and basic accuracy of the witnesses, not necessarily the identity of the light. A person can faithfully describe something highly unfamiliar while misjudging its distance, shape or source.

## The F-111 timeline changed the case

A United States Air Force General Dynamics F-111E, serial number 68-0024, belonging to the 20th Tactical Fighter Wing, was lost near North Crawley later that morning. The aircraft had taken off from RAF Upper Heyford, in Oxfordshire. Its crew eventually escaped in the aircraft’s detachable ejection module, while the abandoned jet crashed into a field. Specialist aviation records identify 68-0024 as the aircraft lost near North Crawley in 1973.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aviationphotocompany.com/p270284351/h4B2E5F9F" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aviationphotocompany.com">[The Aviation Photo Company]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aviationphotocompany.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Aviation Photo CompanyThe Aviation Photo Company &#124; F-111/EF-111 Aardvark/Raven (General Dynamics) &#124; USAF 20 TFW General Dynamics F-11...</span></span></span>

Early discussions tended to compare the sighting at roughly 9am with the final crash or ejection at about 9.47am. On that simplified chronology, the aircraft explanation looked weak: a brief flame or fuel dump near Cuddington could hardly remain visible until a crash roughly 40 minutes later. This timing objection helped keep the UFO interpretation alive and appeared in later local retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://strangethame.co.uk/is-aylesbury-vale-a-ufo-corridor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: strangethame.co.uk">[Strange Thame]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">strangethame.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Strange Thame Is Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Strange ThameIs Aylesbury Vale a UFO corridor? - Strange Thame...</span></span></span>

The important question, however, was not when the aircraft finally crashed. It was when its emergency began.

A retrospective account by investigator Jenny Randles, based on detailed aviation reporting and air-to-ground communications, placed the first trouble at approximately 8.58 and 30 seconds, about two minutes after take-off. A loose fuel-line component was said to have caused a leak and fire around the port engine. Damage then affected the aircraft’s control system. The pilot reported an engine fire, single-engine flight and possible loss of control before confirming that fuel was being dumped.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Northern UFO News No 156</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Northern UFO News No 156</span></span></span>

6. Fuel dumping stopped shortly before 9.04am, after which the aircraft changed direction and climbed.
7. The crew spent more than 40 minutes attempting to regain control and arrange an emergency landing.
8. The ejection and crash occurred only after those prolonged efforts, at approximately 9.47am. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

</div>

This chronology removes the supposed 40-minute contradiction. Day did not need to have filmed the aircraft at the moment it crashed. He could have filmed the opening minutes of an emergency that continued for much longer.

## Why the burning-fuel explanation fits

The F-111 interpretation does more than supply an aircraft somewhere in the region. It reportedly matches several particular features of the observation.

**The timing is close.** The reported sighting began at about 9am, while radio information placed the aircraft’s difficulty and fuel dumping between roughly 8.58am and 9.04am. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

**The locations and direction are compatible.** The damaged aircraft had departed Upper Heyford and was initially flying eastwards, placing it broadly along the relevant Oxfordshire–Buckinghamshire sector. Randles’s later assessment concluded that the aircraft was in the correct area and travelling on the appropriate heading when the fiery object was observed. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

**The colour and appearance are unsurprising for ignited fuel.** A trail of burning fuel viewed end-on, partly obscured by cloud or at considerable distance, need not resemble an aeroplane. The brightest flame can dominate the view while the aircraft itself disappears against an overcast background.

**The apparent sudden disappearance has a straightforward mechanism.** The crew stopped dumping fuel and altered direction. A burning plume seen almost along the aircraft’s line of travel could therefore vanish rapidly when the fuel flow ended, the flame extinguished or the aircraft turned away. The later reconstruction specifically connected the end of the visible phenomenon with the aircraft’s turn and climb into cloud. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

**The lack of loud engine noise is not decisive.** The aircraft was reportedly near the cloud base at about 2,000 feet and had lost one engine. Wind, distance, cloud, traffic noise and the observers’ attention to the bright object could all reduce the apparent sound. Randles argued that the reduced power and atmospheric conditions made it plausible that children in a playground or Day inside his car would not identify a jet by sound. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

The aircraft explanation is not perfect in every reconstructed detail. Publicly accessible accounts do not provide the complete original accident file, precise plotted flight path, all witness statements and original film for independent examination together in one preserved archive. Some timings have also varied between retellings. Even so, the match between the emergency communications and the observation window is much stronger than the earlier comparison with the final crash time.

## Why the dispute lasted

The disagreement endured partly because different people were answering different questions. Peter Day was defending the accuracy of what he had seen and filmed: a strange, silent-looking orange object that did not resemble a conventional aircraft. Sceptical investigators were trying to identify what physical event could create that appearance. Both positions can coexist. Day did not need to mistake an obvious aeroplane for the explanation to be correct; he needed only to see an aircraft-generated flame under conditions that concealed the aircraft.

The investigation also unfolded in stages. A police explanation involving dumped fuel circulated quickly, but without the detailed supporting chronology needed to make it convincing. Day reportedly found little official interest in examining the film, while early UFO researchers lacked access to the complete military accident information. That left a gap in which the later crash was treated as either too late to matter or as a mysterious event possibly connected with the UFO. [Strange Thame](https://strangethame.co.uk/is-aylesbury-vale-a-ufo-corridor/)

Steuart Campbell later promoted the F-111 solution after obtaining accident information, although Randles objected that the aviation explanation had already been considered in earlier research. More detailed articles in *Aircraft Illustrated* reportedly reproduced communications from the emergency and allowed the timing to be matched much more closely. Randles, who had previously entertained unresolved or exotic possibilities, then wrote that the new evidence left no realistic alternative to the burning-fuel explanation. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

That change of position is significant. It was not simply an outside sceptic dismissing a witness account. One of the case’s principal UFO investigators accepted that later evidence had resolved it.

## What the surviving film can and cannot prove

The film remains valuable because it confirms that Day was reacting to a visible luminous phenomenon rather than merely recounting an unsupported memory. It also preserves information about colour, apparent movement, duration and disappearance. In that limited sense, it strengthens the witness evidence.

What it does not provide is enough visual detail to identify the source independently. A small bright image on cine film offers no reliable scale unless its distance is known. A compact orange spot might represent a nearby small object, a distant large flame or a bright plume viewed almost end-on. Without clearly resolved structural features, the film cannot distinguish among those possibilities.

The abrupt change between one frame and the next was sometimes treated as evidence that the object had vanished instantaneously. Later retellings also drew attention to apparent blurring in part of the final image, with speculation about a “force field” or distortion of light. Such an interpretation has no supporting physical evidence and is unnecessary when ordinary photographic effects are considered. Camera movement, film transport, focus, exposure, processing, cloud contrast or a rapid change in the light source could all produce an anomalous-looking frame. The foreground hedge reportedly remaining sharper than more distant trees does not demonstrate that energy was emitted from an object. [Strange Thame](https://strangethame.co.uk/is-aylesbury-vale-a-ufo-corridor/)

The film’s evidential limits are increased by its history. Public accounts generally discuss copies, selected frames or descriptions rather than a modern scan of the original reel accompanied by complete technical documentation. Important information is therefore uncertain or absent, including the exact camera model, lens setting, frame rate, exposure, processing history, generation of the surviving copy and whether any frames were lost during handling.

A rigorous film analysis would need the original reel, high-resolution frame-by-frame scanning, edge and grain inspection, photometric comparison, confirmed camera specifications and a reconstructed line of sight. Even that might describe the light more accurately without establishing its distance. By contrast, the aircraft communications supply time-dependent information that the images alone cannot contain.

## Did the Cuddington film capture a UFO?

In the literal sense, Peter Day filmed something he could not identify at the time, so it was an unidentified flying object to the witness. In the stronger sense usually intended by the question—an object still unexplained after investigation—the evidence now weighs heavily against that conclusion.

The best-supported assessment is that Day and the Chilton witnesses saw burning fuel from F-111E 68-0024 during the opening minutes of its emergency. The reported timings of the engine fire and fuel dump align with the observation; the route and altitude are compatible; and the sudden disappearance can be explained by the fuel dump ending and the aircraft turning or entering cloud. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/Northern_UFO_News_No_156/Northern_UFO_News_No_156_djvu.txt)

The case should therefore be described as plausibly and probably resolved rather than absolutely proved. Its remaining uncertainty comes mainly from incomplete preservation of the original evidence, not from a strong surviving contradiction in the F-111 explanation. There is no credible evidence that an unknown craft caused the aircraft emergency, and the documented mechanical failure removes the need for such a theory.

Within Buckinghamshire’s UFO history, the Cuddington film matters less as photographic proof of something extraordinary than as an unusually clear example of investigative correction. A sincere witness captured a genuinely dramatic sight; an obvious explanation initially appeared not to fit; and later aviation records showed that the crucial event was not the distant crash but the brief fuel dump near the beginning of the emergency.
