## Introduction

[Cardiganshire]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-cardiganshire/' | relative_url }})’s UFO record is best understood as a record of reported experiences rather than a catalogue of proven unknown craft. The surviving evidence consists mainly of witness reports, Ministry of Defence (MoD) files, newspaper coverage and local archival material. These records show that people in the historic county reported unusual lights and objects in the sky, but they rarely contain the kind of independent evidence — such as radar data, physical material or multiple instrument recordings — that could establish an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Cardiganshire_0418d7_ufo_archive_records_1260c9-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Records" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The most valuable archive evidence is therefore not proof that UFOs visited Cardiganshire, but proof of how sightings were recorded, assessed and preserved. The county’s surviving files show the strengths and limits of official investigation: authorities documented what witnesses said, considered possible explanations, and generally found no evidence of a threat to UK airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Government reports and archived cases

### The 2009 Cardigan report in the MoD files

The clearest publicly available Cardiganshire example in official records is a report from Cardigan, listed under the historic county name Cardiganshire in the MoD’s 2009 UFO report collection. On 6 January 2009, a witness reported seeing “five rather big orange things” flying overhead. The entry records the location and description but does not identify the objects or provide supporting evidence that could determine what they were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7582c440f0b6397f35efcb/ufo_report_2009.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report 2009</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reports 2009May 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

This short report illustrates how British UFO records were normally created. The MoD files were primarily a record of reports received, not a scientific investigation database designed to prove or disprove unusual claims. A sighting could remain officially “unidentified” simply because there was insufficient information to decide between possibilities such as aircraft, satellites, balloons, astronomical objects or unusual atmospheric effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

For Cardiganshire researchers, the 2009 entry matters because it provides a fixed archival reference point: a date, location, description and official record trail. At the same time, it does not establish that the witness observed an unknown technology. The document confirms the report, not the cause.

### How Cardiganshire fits into the wider MoD archive

The National Archives holds the surviving MoD UFO files, which include reports from members of the public, correspondence and internal material. The records show that many sightings across Britain involved descriptions of lights, flashes, shapes or moving objects, with investigators often considering ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

The archive system also explains why the surviving record is incomplete. The National Archives notes that before the late 1960s some UFO material was destroyed after set retention periods, meaning not every historic report survives. Later public interest led to greater retention and eventual release of many files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFOs - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

This limitation is important when assessing Cardiganshire’s UFO history. A lack of surviving documents does not necessarily mean that no reports occurred, while the existence of a document does not mean an unexplained event had an extraordinary origin.

### Local newspapers as a second archive trail

Newspapers provide another layer of evidence because they capture public reaction, local discussion and the way sightings entered community memory. Historic Cardiganshire publications, including the *Welsh Gazette* from Aberystwyth and the *Cardigan & Tivy-side Advertiser*, are preserved in searchable newspaper archives, allowing researchers to examine contemporary reporting alongside official files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.findmypast.co.uk/newspapers/wales/cardigan-tivyside-advertiser" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: findmypast.co.uk">[Findmypast]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">findmypast.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Search Cardigan &amp; Tivy-side Advertiser Newspaper Archives Online &#124; Findmypast.co.ukJanuary 14, 1870...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 14, 1870</span></span></span>

Newspaper evidence has a different value from government records. A newspaper report may include fuller witness descriptions, interviews or local speculation, but it was not usually produced through a formal investigation process. For that reason, researchers generally treat press coverage as evidence of a sighting’s social history rather than automatic confirmation of what was observed.

## What official records can prove

Official UFO files are strongest when answering practical questions:

* **Was a report made?**

Yes. Government records can confirm that a person contacted authorities and that the report was logged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O reports in the UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO reports in the UK - GOV.UK...</span></span></span>
* **Where and when was something reported?**

Often yes. MoD reports typically include dates, locations and brief descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O reports in the UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO reports in the UK - GOV.UK...</span></span></span>
* **Was the sighting investigated for defence concerns?**

In some cases, yes. The MoD maintained UFO reporting systems partly because unusual aerial reports could potentially relate to air defence issues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>
* **Do the records prove extraterrestrial craft?**

No. The archives contain reports and assessments, not confirmed evidence of alien vehicles. The National Archives notes that many reports have conventional explanations, while some remain unusual because information is limited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding Cardiganshire’s archive evidence. A recorded “UFO” means an unidentified sighting at the time of reporting. It does not mean that investigators concluded the object was unknown in a scientific sense.

## Missing evidence and unresolved claims

### Why some cases remain open

Many Cardiganshire reports cannot be fully resolved because the available evidence is limited. A witness description may record colour, movement and approximate size, but without photographs, video, precise measurements or independent observers, later researchers have few ways to test competing explanations.

The 2009 Cardigan report demonstrates this problem. The description of orange objects is specific enough to preserve the event in the archive, but too brief to identify the cause. Possible explanations could include aircraft lights, lanterns, astronomical objects or other aerial phenomena, but the surviving record does not allow a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7582c440f0b6397f35efcb/ufo_report_2009.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report 2009</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reports 2009May 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

### The difference between “unresolved” and “unexplained”

Archive research often separates three categories:

**Documented but unresolved:**

A genuine report exists, but the evidence is insufficient to determine what happened.

**Weakly supported claims:**

A story may circulate through local accounts or later retellings, but has little surviving documentation.

**Explained reports:**

Enough information exists to connect the sighting to an ordinary cause such as aircraft, satellites, planets or weather effects. The MoD archive contains many examples where such explanations were considered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

Cardiganshire’s surviving record is mostly found in the first category: documented reports with limited evidence. That makes the archive valuable for understanding public reports and official responses, but not as proof of extraordinary activity.

### What stronger evidence would look like

For a Cardiganshire case to move beyond a simple witness report, researchers would look for evidence such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* independent witnesses describing the same object from different locations;
* photographs or video with reliable timestamps and locations;
* radar or aviation records;
* weather and astronomical checks;
* detailed investigation notes showing why conventional explanations fail.

</div>

The absence of this type of evidence does not prove that every report was ordinary, but it explains why official archives rarely move beyond recording the event.

## Why the archives still matter

Cardiganshire’s UFO files are significant because they preserve a record of encounters between unusual observations and official institutions. They show how residents reported things they could not immediately identify, how government departments handled those reports, and why uncertainty often remained after investigation.

The historical value of these records is therefore less about proving a mysterious cause and more about documenting a recurring human question: what happens when people see something in the sky that they cannot easily explain? In Cardiganshire, the archive evidence provides a careful answer — reports existed, investigations were recorded, and some cases remained unresolved because the evidence stopped short of a definitive explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })

    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
