## Introduction

The county’s importance lies in the combination of factors that can make unusual sightings more likely to be reported: a remote landscape with dark skies, a long coastline, proximity to major air and maritime routes, and the presence of significant industrial and scientific facilities such as [Dounreay]({{ 'dounreay/' | relative_url }}). Historic Caithness geography is the focus here: modern administrative arrangements place the area within the Highland council region, but the historic county remains the useful reference point for tracing older newspapers, local records, and UFO reports.

## Why Caithness attracted UFO reports

Caithness sits at the far north of mainland Britain, with wide horizons and relatively low levels of urban light pollution. For observers, this creates ideal conditions for noticing stars, planets, aircraft lights, satellites, meteors, and atmospheric effects. At the same time, the area has long had aviation and defence connections, making aircraft activity another important part of interpreting reports.

The presence of the Dounreay nuclear research site near Thurso also contributed to local speculation. Nuclear facilities have often become linked in popular culture with UFO stories because they are associated with advanced technology, security restrictions, and unusual-looking infrastructure. ## The Thurso sightings and the 1960s–1970s wave

One of the clearest documented Caithness UFO episodes occurred in November 1967, when the *Caithness Courier* reported that three men in Thurso had seen what they described as a “flying saucer”. The newspaper described it as one of the first such reports from the town and noted that it coincided with other claimed sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results?basicsearch=ufo&amp;mostspecificlocation=thurso%2C+caithness%2C+scotland&amp;place=thurso%2C+caithness%2C+scotland&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false&amp;somesearch=ufo&amp;sortorder=dayearly" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk.</span></span></span>

A more active period followed in the early 1970s. In January 1974, reports from Thurso generated significant local interest after residents described unusual objects in the sky. One widely reported account involved a Dounreay painter, Robert Richardson, who described seeing a horseshoe-shaped object. Further reports followed, including accounts from other local witnesses. The *Caithness Courier* coverage shows that the event developed into a local “flap” — a short period when reports appear to increase and public attention grows.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results?basicsearch=ufo&amp;mostspecificlocation=thurso%2C+caithness%2C+scotland&amp;place=thurso%2C+caithness%2C+scotland&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false&amp;somesearch=ufo&amp;sortorder=dayearly" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The 1974 reports are notable because they demonstrate how UFO cases often develop socially as well as observationally. A first report can encourage others to reinterpret unusual sights, leading to more phone calls, letters, and newspaper attention. That does not mean witnesses were deliberately mistaken; rather, unusual observations can spread through communities when people begin comparing experiences.

## What was investigated and what remains uncertain

The available record for Caithness consists mainly of newspaper accounts and witness statements rather than detailed scientific investigations. There is no publicly available evidence of a Caithness case involving recovered material, confirmed unknown aircraft, or military radar data that remains unexplained.

Local UFO investigators and researchers sometimes classify some Caithness sightings as “unexplained” because a witness description does not immediately identify the object. That category is limited: an unidentified sighting means that the observer or investigator did not establish the cause, not that the object was proven to be unusual. The distinction is central to understanding UK UFO records.

The Ministry of Defence’s historical approach reflects this cautious interpretation. Surviving files contain many reports where officials considered [possible explanations]({{ 'possible-explanations-f6239b/' | relative_url }}) such as astronomical objects, aircraft, or atmospheric effects. The records also show that many reports lacked enough information for a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Possible explanations for Caithness sightings

Several ordinary explanations are relevant when assessing reports from the county.

**Aircraft and military activity**

Northern Scotland has long been used for aviation training and military flying. Aircraft viewed at unusual angles, especially at night, can appear to move strangely or lack obvious shape and sound.

**Astronomical objects**

Bright planets, particularly Venus, have historically been responsible for many UFO reports because they can appear to hover, change brightness, or seem to move when viewed through moving clouds or from a moving vehicle. UK official UFO records specifically mention Venus and other astronomical explanations among common explanations for reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

**Weather and atmospheric effects**

Northern coastal areas can produce unusual visual conditions involving mist, reflections, temperature changes, and distorted views of distant lights.

**Human perception and expectation**

Once a community begins discussing UFO sightings, later observations may be described using similar language. Shapes such as discs, triangles, and glowing objects are influenced by both what is seen and how people interpret ambiguous sights.

## Caithness in the wider Scottish UFO landscape

Caithness is not generally regarded as Scotland’s main UFO hotspot. Other areas, including central Scotland’s Bonnybridge region, have generated far larger numbers of reported incidents and stronger public attention. However, Caithness remains interesting because its reports provide a northern example of how UFO stories develop around local witnesses, newspapers, aviation activity, and unusual landscapes.

The county’s cases also show the difference between an unresolved report and a confirmed mystery. The [Thurso sightings]({{ 'thurso-sightings/' | relative_url }}) of the late 1960s and 1970s remain part of local UFO folklore because witnesses reported genuinely unusual experiences and newspapers preserved the accounts. Yet the surviving evidence does not establish that these events involved technology beyond known human or natural explanations.
