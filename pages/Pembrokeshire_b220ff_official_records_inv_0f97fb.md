## Introduction

The official record shows that the authorities did pay attention to [Pembrokeshire]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-pembrokeshires/' | relative_url }})’s 1977 UFO reports, but it does not show that they confirmed a landed spacecraft, extraterrestrial visitors or even a single cause behind the sightings. Ministry of Defence correspondence treated the reports chiefly as a question of air defence, security and unidentified aircraft. An officer from RAF Brawdy visited the Haven Fort Hotel after one of the most dramatic encounters, while officials considered whether a discreet police inquiry might establish if a practical joker was involved. No publicly available file records verified landing marks, radar evidence, recovered material or an aircraft posing a defence threat.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Pembrokeshire_b220ff_official_records_inv_0f97fb-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Records" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This distinction matters. An official document proves that a report entered government channels; it does not prove that the reported event happened exactly as described. In Pembrokeshire, the surviving papers reveal genuine official uncertainty, a limited inquiry and substantial evidential gaps—not government confirmation of an alien visit.

## What the Ministry of Defence records contain

The Ministry of Defence received many UFO reports during the 1970s, normally through letters from witnesses, MPs, police stations, RAF units or other government departments. Reports from Pembrokeshire were therefore handled within a national administrative system rather than by a dedicated local scientific investigation.

The National Archives holds an open file catalogued as **DEFE 24/1206, “UFO reports and correspondence: April–October 1977”**, followed by **DEFE 24/1207**, covering October 1977 to March 1978. These files belong to the Ministry of Defence’s Defence Secretariat records and preserve the wider stream of reports and correspondence from the period in which the Pembrokeshire wave attracted national attention. The catalogue confirms that the material was retained as a public record, but the existence of a file is not itself evidence that officials accepted the extraordinary interpretation of its contents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/record?catid=8445287&amp;catln=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery National ArchivesUFO reports and correspondence: April-October 1977 &#124; The National ArchivesUFO reports and correspondence: Apri...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1977</span></span></span>

The surviving correspondence indicates that official interest increased after Rosa Granville, who ran the Haven Fort Hotel near Little Haven, reported an object and tall figures outside the hotel. She contacted her MP, Nicholas Edwards, who passed the matter to the Ministry of Defence amid wider concern about reports from the county. A memorandum subsequently asked the RAF’s Provost and Security Service whether a discreet inquiry might be worthwhile and whether there was enough initial evidence to justify a more specialised investigation. The guarded wording is important: officials were asking whether the reports merited further attention, not stating that they had established what had occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://herald.wales/west-wales/pembrokeshire/the-welsh-triangle-what-was-seen-in-the-sky-back-in-1977/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: herald.wales">[Herald.Wales]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">herald.wales</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the welsh triangle what was seen in the sky back in 1977</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 12, 2021 — The Welsh Triangle: What was seen in the sky back in 1977? - Herald.Wales Home » The Welsh Triangle: What was seen in th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2021</span></span></span>

The files also have to be read in the context of the Ministry’s purpose. Its primary concern was whether an unexplained observation might indicate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an unidentified aircraft entering or operating in British airspace;
* activity near an RAF station or other sensitive site;
* unfamiliar military technology;
* a security incident, deception or public-order problem;
* information relevant to air safety or national defence.

</div>

That remit was narrower than the questions later asked by UFO enthusiasts. Officials did not need to solve every strange light or unusual personal experience. Once a report appeared to have no defence significance, there was often little institutional reason to devote further resources to it.

This limited approach was not unique to Pembrokeshire. Research based on the released Ministry of Defence archive describes a large collection of witness accounts, parliamentary correspondence and policy papers, but notes that many routine reports received only a brief assessment before being filed. The government system was designed to identify possible threats, not to run open-ended investigations into every unexplained claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drdavidclarke.co.uk/national-archives-ufo-files-7/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drdavidclarke.co.uk">[drdavidclarke.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drdavidclarke.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives UFO Files &#124;National Archives UFO Files &#124; National Archives UFO Files “...the MoD UFO Desk’s files remains one of the la...</span></span></span>

## What happened at the Haven Fort Hotel

The RAF visit to the Haven Fort Hotel is the clearest known example of direct official action in the Pembrokeshire wave. Granville’s account was unusually dramatic: she described a brilliantly illuminated object near the hotel, intense heat and two tall, featureless figures. Because the report reached the Ministry through an MP, it received more formal attention than many casual sightings.

Flight Lieutenant Cowan from nearby RAF Brawdy visited the hotel and inspected the reported location. Accounts derived from the official correspondence say that he found no convincing physical evidence that a craft had landed. There was no recorded wreckage, technical residue or unambiguous ground trace that could be taken away for laboratory analysis. He also noted local speculation that a practical joker might be responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://herald.wales/west-wales/pembrokeshire/the-welsh-triangle-what-was-seen-in-the-sky-back-in-1977/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: herald.wales">[Herald.Wales]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">herald.wales</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the welsh triangle what was seen in the sky back in 1977</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 12, 2021 — The Welsh Triangle: What was seen in the sky back in 1977? - Herald.Wales Home » The Welsh Triangle: What was seen in th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2021</span></span></span>

The officer reportedly considered whether the description of the figures resembled protective clothing associated with industrial firefighting. Pembrokeshire’s coast included oil installations and related industrial activity, making bulky suits, helmets and large visors a locally available visual model. That observation did not prove that Granville saw a person in such equipment. It was an investigative possibility: a human figure in unfamiliar protective clothing, glimpsed at night under unusual illumination, could appear much stranger than it was.

This is an example of how the official record both strengthens and weakens the story.

It **strengthens** it in the limited sense that Granville made a sufficiently serious complaint to generate correspondence, parliamentary interest and an RAF visit. Her report was not merely invented decades later for a documentary.

It **weakens** the landed-craft interpretation because the site visit produced no publicly recorded physical confirmation. The most dramatic elements remained dependent on witness testimony, while officials identified an ordinary mechanism—protective clothing or deliberate impersonation—that could potentially account for at least some of the “silver figure” reports.

The inquiry was also conducted after the event. By the time an officer reached the location, temporary marks could have disappeared, the scene may have been disturbed and weather conditions may have changed. Failure to find evidence therefore does not demonstrate that nothing happened. It does, however, mean that the official visit cannot be cited as verification of a landing.

## Why the investigation remained so limited

A modern reader may imagine that an RAF investigation would involve radar reconstruction, aviation logs, forensic sampling, astronomical analysis and detailed interviews with every witness. The Pembrokeshire papers do not reveal an operation of that scale.

Several practical limitations restricted what officials could establish.

**The reports were fragmented.** The [Broad Haven school]({{ 'school-sighting/' | relative_url }}) sighting, the Haven Fort Hotel encounter and later reports of lights or figures occurred at different places and times. Grouping them under the “[Welsh Triangle]({{ '1977-wave/' | relative_url }})” label made them appear to be one unfolding event, but officials had no firm basis for assuming that every account had the same cause.

**Much of the evidence was testimonial.** Witness statements can preserve genuine experiences, but they are difficult to test after the event. Estimates of distance, size, speed and height are particularly unreliable when the observer has no fixed reference point. Descriptions can also change through discussion, questioning and repeated media retelling.

**There was little durable physical evidence.** No publicly documented object, instrument reading or trace established the presence of an unconventional machine. Without such evidence, investigators were left comparing descriptions with possible aircraft, astronomical objects, clothing, lights, pranks and misunderstandings.

**The Ministry applied a defence threshold.** A strange observation could remain unexplained without qualifying as a military problem. “Unidentified” often meant that officials lacked enough information to assign a cause, not that they had discovered technology beyond human capability.

**Local records are incomplete.** In response to a Freedom of Information request for Broad Haven UFO and unusual-light reports from 1977, Dyfed-Powys Police stated that it held no relevant information because its available records did not extend back that far. That answer does not establish that police received no reports in 1977; it means the present force could not retrieve surviving records through its current systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dyfed-powys.police.uk/foi-ai/dyfed-powys-police/disclosure2022/october/ufo-and-uap-reports-7142022/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dyfed-powys.police.uk">[dyfed-powys.police.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dyfed-powys.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo and uap reports 7142022</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Touch device users, explore by touch or with swipe ge...</span></span></span>

These gaps make retrospective certainty impossible. They also explain why apparently simple questions—whether radar detected anything, how many statements were officially taken, or whether police linked separate incidents—may not have a surviving documentary answer.

## Does a discreet inquiry imply a cover-up?

The language of secrecy in the correspondence has become one of the most disputed parts of the story. Officials considered a “discreet” inquiry, and one memorandum reportedly stressed that the minister had not yet been told that advice was being sought. To some readers, such wording suggests that the government knew more than it admitted.

A more cautious reading is that discretion was normal when officials were testing an allegation before escalating it. Quiet inquiries can prevent publicity from contaminating witness accounts, avoid embarrassing an MP or minister with an unsupported claim, and reduce the risk of encouraging further hoaxes. RAF police involvement would also have been understandable if officials suspected impersonation, trespass or activity near a military establishment.

There was a real difference between the government’s public language and its internal activity. Public statements often said that UFO reports had no apparent defence significance, while officials still logged them, circulated correspondence and occasionally sought checks from military units. But those positions are not necessarily contradictory. A report could be examined specifically to determine whether it had defence significance and then be judged not to have any.

Nor does classification automatically indicate hidden alien evidence. Defence files can contain personal details, information about military procedures, intelligence responsibilities and the capabilities of air-defence systems. Those subjects can justify restricted handling regardless of what caused the original sighting. Some Ministry of Defence UFO records were later released with redactions, while archive catalogue entries show that privacy restrictions continue to affect a portion of the wider collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C10518747" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery National ArchivesDigital copy of DEFE 24/1977: UFO reports; with redactions &#124; The National ArchivesDigital copy of DEFE 24/1977...</span></span></span>

Secrecy nevertheless had consequences. Sparse public explanations left room for later claims that the RAF had conducted a major covert operation. The surviving evidence supports a more modest conclusion: officials privately took the reports seriously enough to ask questions, but the documented response was limited and did not produce proof of an extraordinary vehicle.

## What the files do—and do not—establish

The official material is most useful when its claims are separated into different levels of evidential strength.

**Clearly established by the record:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* reports from Pembrokeshire reached government and parliamentary channels;
* the Ministry of Defence considered whether further inquiry was justified;
* an RAF Brawdy officer visited the Haven Fort Hotel;
* officials considered human deception or protective clothing among the possibilities;
* no publicly released official finding confirmed an alien craft or visitor.

</div>

**Reasonable but not conclusively established:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* one or more reports may have involved a prank;
* military, industrial or aviation activity may have contributed to the wider wave;
* publicity may have encouraged witnesses to interpret unrelated events within a shared UFO narrative.

</div>

**Not established by the official record:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* that the Broad Haven pupils saw an extraterrestrial spacecraft;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://spookyvalley.com/events/1977-broad-haven-school/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spookyvalley.com">[spookyvalley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spookyvalley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Broad Haven School Sighting &#124; Spooky Valley</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Broad Haven School Sighting &#124; Spooky Valley</span></span></span>
* that a craft landed at the Haven Fort Hotel;
* that radar or military sensors tracked the reported objects;
* that the authorities recovered materials or concealed alien occupants;
* that every sighting in the Pembrokeshire wave had one common cause.

</div>

The absence of proof is not the same as a complete explanation. Some individual observations remain unresolved because the information is too limited, not because officials concluded that they were genuinely anomalous machines. In historical casework, “unresolved” is often a statement about the weakness of the surviving evidence.

## Why later releases did not settle the case

The release of government UFO files gave the Pembrokeshire story a firmer documentary foundation. It demonstrated that official correspondence, RAF involvement and internal discussion were real. That was valuable because later popular accounts sometimes mixed well-attested details with rumours, reconstructed dialogue and claims that could not be traced to contemporary records.

Yet disclosure did not supply the decisive evidence many readers expected. The papers showed officials handling letters, assessing possible defence implications and discussing a low-key inquiry. They did not reveal a hidden scientific report that validated a landed craft.

The Ministry of Defence eventually ended its dedicated collection and investigation of UFO or unidentified aerial phenomena reports in 2009. In parliamentary answers, the department has repeatedly said that more than fifty years of reports produced no sighting indicating a military threat to the United Kingdom, and that resources were therefore redirected to other defence work. It has also stated that relevant UFO files created up to 2009 were transferred to The National Archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2024-12-05/18321" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: questions-statements.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">questions-statements.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament Written questions and answers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Parliament Written questions and answers</span></span></span>

That national policy does not retrospectively explain Pembrokeshire. It does clarify what official investigation was intended to achieve. The Ministry was not trying to prove or disprove extraterrestrial life. It was asking whether reports represented hostile aircraft, dangerous technology or another matter requiring defence action.

On that narrower question, the Pembrokeshire record reached a practical conclusion: officials found no publicly demonstrable threat and no physical proof requiring a major specialist investigation. On the broader question—what every witness actually saw—the archive remains incomplete.

## The most defensible judgement

The official files make the 1977 Pembrokeshire wave more historically substantial, but not more conclusive as evidence of alien visitation. They confirm that reports were made, that an MP pressed the issue, that the Ministry of Defence consulted the RAF and that an officer inspected the site of one major claim. They also show how quickly official attention turned towards possible pranks, protective clothing and the absence of physical traces.

The most defensible interpretation is therefore neither “the government proved it was a hoax” nor “secret files prove aliens landed”. The inquiry was too limited to establish a single explanation for the whole wave, while the available evidence was too weak to justify the extraordinary conclusion of a landed extraterrestrial craft.

Pembrokeshire remains an important British UFO case because of the number and persistence of its witnesses, not because official records solved it. The files preserve a revealing encounter between local testimony and the machinery of government: unusual claims were logged and cautiously checked, but once they failed to cross the threshold of demonstrable defence significance, investigation largely stopped.

    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
