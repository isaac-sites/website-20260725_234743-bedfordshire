## Introduction

The Ballykelly and Bellaghy sightings are memorable because the witnesses described sudden movement, unusual shapes and strong emotional reactions. They are not, however, strongly evidenced UFO cases. Ballykelly rests on one student’s account of several apparently structured objects in September 2013. Bellaghy concerns a flashing blue light seen in December 2011 by people travelling in a car between Portglenone and Bellaghy. Neither report is supported by photographs, radar records, aircraft checks, precise sightline measurements or a documented investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/449546/numerous-ufo-sightings-reported-in-county-derry-over-the-past-few-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: derrynow.com">[Derry Now]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">derrynow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on derrynow.com.</span></span></span>

The comparison is useful precisely because the reports fail in different ways. Ballykelly contains descriptive detail but is shaped by a solitary observer’s expectations and fear. Bellaghy has more than one potential witness, yet the observation was made from a moving vehicle, where perspective and uncertain distance can make an ordinary light appear to dart or stop. The fairest assessment is that both remain unidentified at the level of the surviving testimony, but neither provides convincing evidence of an extraordinary craft.

## The Ballykelly student’s structured-object account

The Ballykelly report dates to September 2013 and was later summarised by *Derry Now* from material submitted to a UFO-reporting website. The witness, described as a student living in Ballykelly, claimed to have seen four “crafts”. One reportedly resembled a traffic cone; another was compared with a modern set of bathroom scales—oval, but curved through the middle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/449546/numerous-ufo-sightings-reported-in-county-derry-over-the-past-few-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: derrynow.com">[Derry Now]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">derrynow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on derrynow.com.</span></span></span>

People commonly describe unfamiliar aerial appearances by comparing them with recognisable household objects. Britain’s archived UFO files contain similarly improvised descriptions, including objects likened to an upturned cup, a teapot lid or a serving dish. These analogies are useful records of appearance, but they are not measurements of physical structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives holds files on UFO reports and correspondence released by the Ministry of Defence, we’re all aware of that.... Without distance information, even the word “craft” goes beyond what the observation can demonstrate. A small object nearby, a large object far away and a pattern of lights against darkness can occupy the same apparent area of sky. Shape estimates are especially fragile when only bright or dark outlines are visible.

Ballykelly’s location adds an aviation connection in the broad historical sense. The village is in historic [County Londonderry]({{ 'what-really-flew-over-county-londonderry/' | relative_url }}) and was associated with the former RAF Ballykelly, later Shackleton Barracks. That history makes aircraft an obvious category to check, but it does not itself make the 2013 report military or radar-related. ### Emotion as part of the evidence

The most revealing part of the Ballykelly testimony may be the witness’s emotional sequence. The student described initially feeling “peace and a sort of inner understanding”, then becoming frightened and going indoors because, despite an existing interest in UFOs and extraterrestrials, they did not want to be abducted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/449546/numerous-ufo-sightings-reported-in-county-derry-over-the-past-few-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: derrynow.com">[Derry Now]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">derrynow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on derrynow.com.</span></span></span>

That statement should not be mocked or treated as proof of invention. It records a genuine subjective response. It does, however, show that the witness interpreted the event through an already familiar UFO and abduction framework while it was happening. The fear therefore cannot be used independently to prove that the objects were threatening, intelligently controlled or extraterrestrial. It may instead reveal how prior beliefs supplied meaning where visual information was incomplete.

The wording preserved by the later newspaper article also raises a source-quality problem. The public record is not a contemporaneous interview conducted under controlled conditions. It is a compressed retelling of a self-submitted internet account. Police guidance on witness evidence stresses the value of recording people’s own words, allowing uncertainty and avoiding questions that push them towards greater precision than their memory supports. Those safeguards are not visible here.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.college.police.uk/guidance/obtaining-initial-accounts/witnesses-own-words-and-open-questioning" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: college.police.uk">[College of Policing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">college.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Evidence also suggested that when witnesses are made to give more specific answers to questions they are less accurate than when they are...</span></span></span>

The Ballykelly claim is therefore vivid but evidentially weak. Its detail makes the story memorable; it does not compensate for the absence of corroboration or basic observational data.

## The flashing blue light near Bellaghy

The Bellaghy-area report concerns a journey on 18 December 2011 between Portglenone and Bellaghy. According to the surviving account, the witness’s wife was driving, two 12-year-old children were in the back seat, and a flashing blue light appeared ahead. It reportedly travelled across the view just above the horizon, stopped for roughly two seconds, moved again towards what the witness called the seven-o’clock position, paused briefly and disappeared. The account stated that visibility was clear and that no noise was heard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.seniorennet.be/peter2011/archief.php?startdatum=1322694000&amp;stopdatum=1325372400" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.seniorennet.be">[blog.seniorennet.be]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.seniorennet.be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image Victoria Lines -- Dirk Vander Ploeg received an email from a UFO Digest reader that took photos in the center on the island of Malt...</span></span></span>

Geographically, the journey began around Portglenone, on the County Antrim side of the River Bann, and headed towards Bellaghy in historic County Londonderry. The exact observation point was not recorded publicly, so it cannot confidently be assigned to one side of the county boundary. It belongs on the County Londonderry page because Bellaghy and the direction of travel form part of the report, but the uncertainty matters when mapping the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gazetteer.org.uk/place/Bellaghy%2C_Londonderry_322018" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gazetteer.org.uk">[Gazetteer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gazetteer.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gazetteer.org.uk.</span></span></span>

Bellaghy has a limited advantage over Ballykelly because several people were apparently present. Yet the available wording does not show that each passenger independently observed the full sequence or gave a separate account. The driver first drew attention to the light, but the published narrative appears to come from one reporter speaking for the group. Multiple occupants are not equivalent to multiple independent testimonies, particularly when they discuss the event together before any statements are recorded.

Research-based police guidance warns that discussion between co-witnesses can alter recall, introduce details from another person and reduce the independence of later accounts. No allegation of deliberate embellishment is needed: ordinary conversation can merge several memories into one shared version.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.college.police.uk/guidance/obtaining-initial-accounts/clarifying-sources-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: college.police.uk">[College of Policing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">college.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">College of Policing Clarifying sources of information &#124; College of Policing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">College of Policing Clarifying sources of information &#124; College of Policing</span></span></span>

## Why motion from a car is difficult to judge

The Bellaghy description places great weight on movement: the light “shot across”, stopped and then shot in another direction. Those observations sound difficult to reconcile with an aircraft if they are treated as measured changes in the object’s real velocity. The problem is that the report provides only movement across the witnesses’ field of view.

A point of light against a dark or empty sky gives few dependable distance cues. Without knowing whether it was tens of metres away or many kilometres away, its true size and speed cannot be calculated. Apparent movement may also combine the light’s motion with the movement and changing direction of the witnesses’ car. Turning along a road, passing trees or hedges, changes in head position and temporary obstruction can all make a distant light appear to slide, halt or vanish.

Night-driving research confirms that reduced contrast and poor illumination affect judgements of speed and distance. Studies do not imply that every unusual light is an illusion, but they do show why confident descriptions of rapid motion should not be treated as instrument-quality measurements. Low-contrast visual scenes can distort perceived speed, while darkness makes it harder to establish how far away an object is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26456-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nature.com.</span></span></span>

The clock-face language in the report adds another uncertainty. Positions such as nine o’clock and seven o’clock describe where the light appeared relative to the car or observer, not a fixed bearing over the landscape. If the vehicle changed heading, the reference frame changed as well. A reconstruction would require the road used, travel direction, vehicle speed, exact viewing times and fixed landmarks. None is supplied.

The brief pauses are similarly difficult to assess. A distant aircraft turning towards the observer may show little sideways motion. A rotating beacon can disappear and reappear. An object may pass behind roadside features too dark for the occupants to notice. Blue emergency lighting, aircraft lighting, a distant ground source, reflections or an astronomical object viewed through broken cloud would all require checking. The record is too thin to select one explanation confidently.

“No noise” is also less decisive than it first appears. A closed or moving car produces engine, tyre and airflow noise, while a distant aircraft may be visible without being clearly audible. Silence would be important in a close-range observation with a secure distance estimate; here, the distance is unknown.

## What later reporting changed

The principal later development was publicity, not new evidence. *Derry Now* brought both accounts to a wider County Londonderry audience in April 2017, several years after the alleged events. Its article accurately preserved some colourful details, but it did not report fresh interviews, locate photographs, obtain air-traffic information or reconstruct either sightline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/449546/numerous-ufo-sightings-reported-in-county-derry-over-the-past-few-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: derrynow.com">[Derry Now]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">derrynow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on derrynow.com.</span></span></span>

The same article quoted a Northern Ireland UFO Society representative who noted that many reports turn out to involve lanterns, satellites, aircraft, planets or drones. That general caution is sensible, although it does not identify either Ballykelly or Bellaghy specifically. Claims in the article about unusual activity around “natural energy” sites are not accompanied by testable evidence and do not strengthen these two reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/449546/numerous-ufo-sightings-reported-in-county-derry-over-the-past-few-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: derrynow.com">[Derry Now]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">derrynow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on derrynow.com.</span></span></span>

Later repetition may actually weaken confidence if readers mistake circulation for corroboration. The Bellaghy wording appears across derivative UFO pages, but the repeated versions trace back to the same submitted narrative rather than to new witnesses. The Ballykelly account likewise survives chiefly through a newspaper summary of an online report. Multiple web appearances therefore do not create multiple sources of observation.

## The most balanced verdict

The Ballykelly and Bellaghy witnesses may sincerely have seen things they could not identify. There is no positive evidence that either story was fabricated, and both contain details worth preserving within County Londonderry’s UFO history. Sincerity, however, cannot establish distance, physical shape or real speed.

Ballykelly is the weaker claim because it relies on one person, lacks basic measurements and openly blends observation with an existing extraterrestrial-abduction narrative. Bellaghy is somewhat more restrained and involved several people, but it remains weak because the decisive motion was judged from a moving vehicle and no independent accounts or fixed bearings survive.

Both are best classified as **unresolved but poorly evidenced witness reports**, not as demonstrated encounters with extraordinary aircraft. Their lasting value is explanatory rather than evidential: they show how unfamiliar lights, uncertain distance, apparent motion, emotion and memory can combine to produce an account that feels compelling to those present while remaining impossible to verify later.
