## Introduction

The Tomintoul mystery is [Banffshire]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-banffshire-skies/' | relative_url }})’s best-known example of a local strange-light story that was later absorbed into UFO literature. The reported event is said to have occurred around 1930 near Tomintoul, then within the historic county of Banffshire, when two men reportedly saw a bright white light and figures apparently moving within it. The story is unusual because it was not originally presented as a flying-saucer case: it emerged through Scottish ghost-story and folklore writing before later researchers reinterpreted it as a possible UFO encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

For UFO history, the importance of Tomintoul lies less in proving that an unidentified craft was present and more in showing how older folklore can change meaning over time. A tale of a mysterious apparition, recorded decades after the alleged event, became classified by some UFO writers as an early “close encounter” account. The limited documentation, lack of contemporary records, and uncertain chain of reporting mean the case remains a piece of local mystery folklore rather than a well-established UFO incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

## The reported 1930 encounter

The Tomintoul account is generally dated to around 1930. Later catalogues describe two men, Alexander Irvine and a companion, walking along Smiddy Lane at about 9.30 pm when they noticed a white light resembling a meteor. As the light became brighter, the men reportedly saw a number of shapes or figures moving within it. The witnesses did not originally interpret the event as an extraterrestrial encounter; the story was associated instead with a ghostly appearance and local beliefs surrounding a deceased nun.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding the case. The modern term “UFO” suggests a technological mystery, but the Tomintoul story belongs first to a much older tradition of unexplained lights, apparitions and supernatural interpretations of unusual sights. The reported elements — a glowing object, moving forms, and a connection to a death omen — fit more closely with the language of folklore than with the structured witness reports collected during later UFO investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

The geography also shaped the story’s later identity. Tomintoul is a Highland village in the historic county of Banffshire, although it is now within the Moray council area. This historic-county setting matters because the UFO project uses Banffshire as a historical area rather than a modern administrative boundary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomintoul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span> A remote Highland landscape, nighttime conditions and longstanding local traditions created a setting in which unusual observations could naturally become part of community storytelling.

## How the story entered UFO literature

The Tomintoul case did not begin with a police report, military investigation or scientific inquiry. Its route into UFO writing appears to have been through Alasdair Alpin MacGregor’s 1955 book *The Ghost Book: Strange Hauntings in Britain*, a collection of supernatural and folklore material rather than a UFO study. MacGregor was a Scottish writer and photographer known for books on folklore, travel and unusual traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Ghost_Book.html?id=SUf8zwEACAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books The Ghost Book: Strange Hauntings in Britain</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksThe Ghost Book: Strange Hauntings in Britain - Alasdair Alpin MacGregor - Google Books...</span></span></span>

Later UFO researchers noticed that the account contained features that could be reframed in UFO terms: a bright object, apparent occupants or figures, and a strange encounter with witnesses. Peter Rogerson, a British UFO researcher, included the story in catalogues of unusual encounters, and later UFO writers repeated the case through those secondary sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

This process illustrates a wider issue in historical UFO research: older reports are sometimes reclassified according to modern expectations. A nineteenth- or early twentieth-century ghost story may later appear in UFO databases because it contains elements that resemble later UFO narratives. That does not necessarily mean the original witnesses described an aircraft or visitors from elsewhere; it may show how interpretations change as cultural ideas develop.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

The Tomintoul story is therefore valuable as a study of interpretation itself. The same account can be read in different ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* **Folklore interpretation:** a supernatural tale involving a strange light, figures and a death-related omen.
* **UFO interpretation:** an early report of an unusual aerial object with apparent occupants.
* **Historical interpretation:** an example of how local legends can be absorbed into later mystery traditions.

</div>

## Evidence gaps and alternative explanations

The strongest limitation of the Tomintoul case is the lack of primary evidence. There is no known photograph, physical trace, radar record, official investigation file or contemporary newspaper report confirming the event. The main written source appears many years after the alleged sighting, meaning researchers cannot easily test the original details or establish how accurately the story was preserved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

The delayed recording also creates uncertainty about details. Memory, storytelling traditions and later editing can all influence how unusual experiences are remembered and transmitted. In this case, the transformation from a reported apparition into a UFO case happened through later collectors and cataloguers rather than through a documented investigation close to the time of the alleged event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/ce3/1930-00-00-uk-tomintoulf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: URECAT-001687 - 1930, Tomintoul, Banffshire, R-U., Alexander Irvine et un amiSeptember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 2018</span></span></span>

Several explanations remain possible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **A folklore account:** The simplest interpretation is that Tomintoul was a traditional ghost story later given a UFO label because of the description of a light and figures.
* **A misidentified natural or atmospheric event:** A bright light seen at night could have had a mundane explanation, although the absence of detailed original testimony makes a specific identification difficult.
* **A genuine unexplained observation:** Some readers may regard the report as unresolved because the surviving account does not provide enough information to determine exactly what was seen.

</div>
