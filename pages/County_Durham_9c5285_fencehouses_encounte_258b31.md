## Introduction

The Fencehouses encounter of 3 September 1976 is probably the most dramatic UFO story associated with historic [County Durham]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-county-durhams/' | relative_url }}). Two women reportedly approached a small oval object on former colliery land, saw doll-sized beings inside it and experienced silence, stopped watches and other unusual effects. Yet the case’s evidential record is strikingly out of proportion to its extraordinary detail. The surviving public account is largely a later summary that cites a short report in *Flying Saucer Review*, rather than a complete investigation file containing original statements, photographs, site plans, instrument tests or named corroborating witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

That does not prove that the women invented their experience. It means the event cannot now be tested to the standard its claims require. Fencehouses therefore matters less as evidence that a craft landed than as a clear example of how a vivid close-encounter narrative can become locally significant while the documents needed to assess it remain missing.

## What the two witnesses said they saw

The reported encounter began at about 9pm, when two women aged 63 and 18 were walking home after visiting a friend in Fencehouses. The weather was described as cool and dry. On an area of waste ground associated with a former mine shaft, they noticed an object that appeared to be resting close to the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

According to the account later published by the British UFO Research Association, or BUFORA, the object was approximately five feet long and three feet high. It was oval, with a glass-like main body, an orange-coloured upper section and what looked like chrome or steel runners beneath it. These dimensions would make it unusually small for a vehicle supposedly carrying occupants: closer to the size of a large domestic appliance or compact cabin than a conventional aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

The older woman was said to have approached closely enough to touch its side, which felt warm. The women then reportedly saw two figures inside. These were described as about the size of large dolls, with long white hair, large eyes and claw-like hands. Frightened, the witnesses moved away. The object supposedly departed at high speed with a humming sound, after an episode lasting roughly ten minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

Several features make the report unusually striking even within UFO literature:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object was supposedly on or immediately above the ground;
* both witnesses were said to have approached it rather than viewing a distant light;
* one witness allegedly made physical contact;
* apparent occupants were visible at close range;
* the object had a structured form, runners and differentiated materials;
* unusual environmental and electrical effects accompanied the sighting.

</div>

This concentration of detail is why Fencehouses is sometimes treated as a “close encounter of the third kind”, meaning a report in which occupants or beings are allegedly observed in association with an unidentified object. That label classifies the testimony; it does not establish that the beings or vehicle physically existed.

## Claims of beings, silence and stopped watches

The most important parts of the story are not merely the unusual-looking object and occupants. The witnesses also reported effects that, had they been documented promptly and independently, might have provided evidence beyond personal recollection.

The older woman claimed that nearby traffic and wind noise abruptly disappeared as she approached the object. Normal sound reportedly returned when the witnesses moved away. Both women’s watches were also said to have stopped near the object and begun working again afterwards. The following day, the older witness allegedly found that an unexplained force prevented her from inserting a vacuum-cleaner plug into a socket, although her daughter could do so.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

These claims increase the story’s apparent strangeness, but each raises unanswered questions.

**The silence:** The surviving account does not show that anyone outside the immediate encounter noticed traffic noise stopping. Nor does it describe a recording, sound measurement or interruption affecting nearby homes. During fear or intense concentration, people can become less aware of background sound. Without independent observations, it is impossible to distinguish an external acoustic effect from a subjective impression remembered after an alarming experience.

**The watches:** Stopped timepieces could have been valuable physical evidence if their make, condition and exact readings had been recorded immediately. A competent examination might have established whether they were mechanical or electronic, whether both failed at the same moment and whether either showed magnetisation or damage. No such test results accompany the accessible account. The statement that the watches “worked normally again” also leaves unclear whether they restarted by themselves, were reset or were simply noticed later to be running.

**The warm surface:** Warmth might indicate machinery, recent solar heating, industrial equipment or merely a subjective judgement made during a brief touch. No recorded surface temperature, burn, residue or material sample survives in the available narrative.

**The vacuum-cleaner plug:** This is the least testable alleged effect. There is no description of the socket, plug, appliance fault or any electrical inspection. The daughter’s ability to insert the plug could point to something as ordinary as alignment, stiffness or the older woman’s physical reaction after a frightening event. Treating it as evidence of a lingering “force” requires assumptions unsupported by documented testing.

The beings themselves present a similar problem. “Long white hair”, “large eyes” and “claw-like hands” are memorable descriptors, but the public summary supplies no separate witness drawings or individual accounts showing whether the two women independently gave the same description. Without separate statements, apparent agreement may simply reflect how an investigator or editor combined their recollections.

## Why the location matters

Fencehouses developed around coal mining and railway activity. The reported site was waste ground connected with a former mine shaft, an environment in which industrial debris, machinery, reflective material and uneven terrain could potentially complicate perception after dark. This does not furnish a specific explanation, but it makes the exact site plan and contemporary photographs especially important.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.disused-stations.org.uk/f/fencehouses/index.shtml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disused-stations.org.uk">[disused-stations.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disused-stations.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Disused Stations:Fencehouses Station</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 18, 2017 — Disused Stations:Fencehouses Station Station Name: FENCEHOUSES [...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 18, 2017</span></span></span>

The village’s geography also needs careful handling. Fencehouses lies across a present-day administrative boundary: its western area remains within County Durham, while its eastern part is within Sunderland. Historically, however, the settlement belongs to County Durham, which is why the encounter fits within the county’s UFO history even when modern sources use Tyne and Wear or Sunderland. Durham County Council’s recent settlement study confirms that the council boundary runs through the community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.durham.gov.uk/media/47409/Woodstone-Village-and-Fencehouses-Settlement-Character-Study/pdf/WoodstoneFencehousesSettlementCharacterStudy.pdf?m=1755101344587" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: durham.gov.uk">[Durham County Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">durham.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Durham County CouncilWoodstone Fencehouses Settlement Character StudyWoodstone Fencehouses Settlement Character Study Set lement Characte...</span></span></span>

Unfortunately, the surviving UFO account does not precisely identify the patch of waste ground, direction of approach, viewing angles or departure path. That omission prevents useful checks such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* establishing the witnesses’ distance from nearby roads and houses;
* determining whether vehicle headlights or railway activity were visible;
* identifying what structures or abandoned equipment stood on the land;
* locating other people who should have seen or heard a rapidly departing object;
* examining whether the claimed object could physically have occupied the stated site.

</div>

A description such as “waste land from a mine shaft” is enough to create atmosphere, but not enough to reconstruct an incident.

## The missing contemporary record

The current BUFORA article identifies its source for Fencehouses as pages 2–3 of *Flying Saucer Review*, volume 22, number 6, published in 1976. That citation is useful because it shows that the story entered UFO literature relatively close to the alleged event. It does not, by itself, reveal who first investigated the case, when the witnesses were interviewed or what supporting documents were collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

The accessible source trail lacks several items normally expected from a strong close-encounter investigation:

* dated and signed statements from each witness;
* the witnesses’ names or a clear explanation of their anonymity;
* separate interviews demonstrating independent agreement;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a map, grid reference or photographs of the site;
* drawings made directly by the witnesses;
* watchmakers’ reports or identification of the watches;
* examination of the electrical socket and vacuum cleaner;
* photographs of marks, impressions or disturbed ground;
* testimony from motorists, residents or other people nearby;
* police, fire-service, civil aviation or military correspondence;
* a documented search for conventional objects or activities at the site.

</div>

None of these omissions proves that no investigation occurred. Some material may have been retained privately, lost, discarded or published in a source that is not readily accessible online. The narrower and more defensible conclusion is that such evidence is not present in the public account now carrying most of the story.

That distinction matters. A case can be old and poorly archived without being false. Equally, age cannot be used to turn missing evidence into positive evidence. Once original records disappear, later summaries become vulnerable to compression, altered wording and accidental embellishment.

## Did official files record the encounter?

Britain’s Ministry of Defence collected UFO reports chiefly to consider whether unidentified activity posed a defence concern. The National Archives now holds extensive Ministry files, including report collections released decades later. Those records are not a national catalogue of every story investigated by civilian UFO groups, and an event’s absence from a known Ministry file would not show that it did not happen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C10518747" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Digital copy of DEFE 24/1977: UFO reports; with redactions &#124; The National ArchivesDigital copy of DEFE 24/1977: UFO reports; wit...</span></span></span>

No accessible official document has emerged alongside the Fencehouses narrative showing that the Ministry of Defence, RAF, police or Civil Aviation Authority investigated the alleged landing. The familiar account instead circulates through civilian UFO literature and BUFORA’s retrospective article. This is significantly weaker than a case supported by contemporaneous police logs, official correspondence or multiple reports collected independently.

The distinction is sometimes blurred in UFO discussion. “Reported in 1976” may sound as though the event was reported immediately to an authority and formally investigated. What can presently be established is more limited: a short version appeared in a 1976 UFO periodical, and a modern BUFORA account repeats it while citing that periodical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

## Could it have been a hoax or misidentification?

There is not enough information to select a convincing ordinary explanation. The story is too poorly documented to test one properly.

A deliberate hoax is possible in principle, but the public record contains no demonstrated confession, exposed prop or evidence of financial motive. It would therefore be unjustified to label the women liars. The anonymity of the witnesses, however, prevents modern checks on their backgrounds, later statements and consistency.

Misidentification is also possible, particularly on former industrial land after sunset. A small cabin, discarded equipment, illuminated vehicle, machinery or reflective structure could conceivably have supplied part of the visual stimulus. Yet the account gives too little positional information to match it to any particular object. The claimed occupants and rapid departure would then require errors of perception, memory, embellishment or a combination of these.

A psychological interpretation need not imply dishonesty. Two people in an unfamiliar or frightening situation can influence one another’s interpretation, especially if they discuss the event before being interviewed separately. Memory is reconstructive: later retellings can become more coherent and specific without conscious invention. The absence of original interview material means this possibility cannot be measured in the Fencehouses case.

There is also no secure basis for treating the stopped watches, silence and plug problem as proof of an electromagnetic field. That explanation is sometimes attached to UFO reports after the fact, but Fencehouses has no surviving measurements showing radiation, magnetism, electrical disruption or equipment damage.

The responsible verdict is therefore not “explained”, because no particular explanation has been demonstrated. It is “weakly evidenced”: the narrative remains unusual, but the available material is inadequate to decide what produced it.

## Later reporting has not strengthened the case

BUFORA’s modern retelling preserves the Fencehouses story and provides a traceable periodical reference, but it does not add new primary evidence. It supplies no recovered witness testimony, photographs, technical examinations or independent corroboration. Instead, it places Fencehouses beside close-encounter reports made in Brazil and Italy on the same date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/three-close-encounter-ufo-reports-on-the-same-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">There were good examples of thes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Three &#x27;Close Encounter&#x27; UFO Reports -on the Same DayMarch 4, 2025 — proximity sightings described as sometimes reported, or when an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

That same-day comparison is interesting as UFO folklore, but it does not strengthen the County Durham event. The reports occurred in widely separated countries, involved different circumstances and are not linked by evidence of a common object, cause or reporting channel. Coinciding calendar dates can appear meaningful after cases have been selected from a worldwide catalogue, particularly when the total number of reports and the criteria used to search them are not stated.

The later article also interprets unusual “force” effects by comparing them with features of other UFO stories. Such parallels may help classify narratives, but they cannot substitute for physical testing in the original case. Similarity between claims shows that witnesses and writers use recurring motifs; it does not establish that a single unknown mechanism produced them.

In evidential terms, the passing decades have weakened rather than strengthened Fencehouses. Potential witnesses have become harder to locate, the industrial landscape has changed and memories can no longer be checked against immediate records. The story has survived, but the supporting case file has not surfaced with it.

## What would change the assessment?

Fencehouses could be reassessed if substantial contemporary material were recovered. The most valuable discovery would be the original investigator’s file, particularly separate witness statements dated shortly after 3 September 1976. Even after half a century, several types of record could clarify the case:

1. **The original *Flying Saucer Review* report and its provenance.** Its wording might identify the investigator, the date of inquiry and whether details now presented as established were first-hand or second-hand.

## How the Fencehouses case should be classified

Fencehouses deserves a place in County Durham’s UFO history because of the specificity and closeness of the alleged encounter. It is not merely a distant light in the sky: it is a claim involving a landed object, occupants, physical contact and effects on the witnesses’ surroundings and possessions.

Those same features demand an unusually high evidential standard. On the material currently accessible, the case does not meet it. The essential account comes through UFO publications; the witnesses remain unnamed; independent statements and technical examinations are absent; and no official investigation record has been publicly tied to the event.

The fairest classification is **unresolved but weakly documented**. “Unresolved” recognises that no ordinary explanation has been established. “Weakly documented” recognises that the surviving testimony cannot be independently reconstructed or tested. It should not be described as a confirmed landing, a proven encounter with beings or a demonstrated electromagnetic event.

Fencehouses is memorable precisely because its details are so extraordinary. Its lasting lesson, however, lies in the gap between narrative richness and evidential depth: the story tells readers almost exactly what supposedly happened, while the surviving record provides too little information to determine whether it happened that way at all.
