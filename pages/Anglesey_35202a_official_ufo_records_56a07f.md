## Introduction

Official records do not show evidence of extraterrestrial activity in [Anglesey]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-angleseys-ufo/' | relative_url }}), but they do show how unusual aerial reports from the island were handled, recorded and assessed. The Ministry of Defence (MoD) collected UFO reports because an unidentified object could represent many things: an aircraft, weather phenomenon, satellite, foreign technology, or simply a sighting that could not be resolved from the information available. The official record is therefore valuable not because it proves UFO claims, but because it shows what was reported, what checks were made and what remained uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFOs - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## MoD reporting and assessment process

The UK government’s UFO records were not designed as a search for proof of alien spacecraft. The MoD’s working meaning of “UFO” was an object in the sky that was seen but not identified. Reports were assessed primarily from a defence and aviation perspective: could the sighting indicate a threat to UK airspace, unusual military activity, or something that needed further attention?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFOs - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

The surviving files show a practical process rather than a specialist hunt for unexplained phenomena. Reports could come from members of the public, police officers, pilots or other officials. Details such as location, time, weather conditions, direction of travel, appearance and duration were recorded where available. Some reports were passed through other bodies, including aviation authorities, before reaching defence officials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

The importance of this process for Anglesey is that the island has a strong aviation connection through [RAF Valley]({{ 'raf-valley/' | relative_url }}). Military flying activity, training aircraft and other aviation movements form part of the normal background against which unusual sightings are reported. RAF Valley itself handles flying-related enquiries from Anglesey residents, reflecting the continuing presence of military aviation in the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/our-organisation/stations/raf-valley/flying-info/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Air Force Flying Info &#124; RAF Valley &#124; Royal Air Force</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Air Force Flying Info &#124; RAF Valley &#124; Royal Air Force</span></span></span> This does not explain every sighting automatically, but it is an essential part of assessing reports from the island.

### The 1977 Bodorgan report and the limits of the record

The best-known Anglesey UFO report involving an official figure is the 1977 [Bodorgan case]({{ 'bodorgan-case/' | relative_url }}). According to later UFO research records, off-duty police officer PC John Owen reported seeing a structured, dome-shaped object from his home near Bodorgan, and the account states that checks were made with RAF Valley regarding night flights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/pdf/PRUFOSREPORT.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[ufocasebook.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE 4TH ANNUALNovember 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2025</span></span></span>

The case is notable because the witness was a police officer and because an attempt was reportedly made to check an ordinary aviation explanation. However, it should not be confused with an official confirmation that an unknown craft was present. The available public record does not contain radar evidence, photographs, physical material or a released MoD investigation file establishing what the object was. The case therefore remains a witness report with unresolved elements rather than a confirmed unexplained event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/pdf/PRUFOSREPORT.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[ufocasebook.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE 4TH ANNUALNovember 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2025</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding official UFO records. A report can be genuine in the sense that a person genuinely observed something unusual, while the cause may remain unknown because the evidence is incomplete.

## What official records can and cannot prove

The release of MoD UFO files through The National Archives has made it easier to examine how British authorities treated these reports. The archive collection contains sighting reports, correspondence and policy material, but the records often show ordinary explanations being considered, including aircraft, satellites, balloons and astronomical objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFOs - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

For Anglesey, official records are most useful in answering practical questions:

* **Was a report recorded?** In several cases, yes. Anglesey appears in published MoD UFO report lists, including sightings recorded in later years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/16851/ufo_report_2008.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report 2008</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reports 2008August 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 31, 2024</span></span></span>

* **Do the files prove alien visitation?** No. The records show reports and assessments, not proof of extraterrestrial vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFOs - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

A common misunderstanding is that a report remaining “unidentified” means investigators believed it was extraordinary. In official terminology, unidentified often meant that the available information was insufficient to identify the object. A lack of explanation is not the same as evidence for a particular explanation.
