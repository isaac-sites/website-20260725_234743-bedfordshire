## Introduction

[Dorset]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-dorsets-ufo/' | relative_url }}) Police recorded 21 reports matching UFO-related search terms between April 2014 and April 2024. The entries include red, orange and flashing lights, supposed spaceships, glowing objects and, in the most striking example, about 18 “football size black triangles” reported over Poole in October 2023. Yet the disclosure does not establish that any of these objects remained unexplained after investigation. Most entries are only one-line summaries of what callers said, with no photographs, officer observations, radar data or documented conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset PoliceOctober 7, 2024 — UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police Your area UFO Sightings Freedom of Information...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2024</span></span></span>

The logs are valuable because they provide a rare official record of what people reported across the Dorset Police area. They are not, however, a verified catalogue of unusual aircraft. Their strongest lesson is about reporting patterns and police record-keeping: residents repeatedly interpret unfamiliar lights as possible UFOs, while the available information is usually too limited to determine what was actually present.

## The 21 reports recorded from 2014 to 2024

The principal dataset comes from a Dorset Police Freedom of Information disclosure released in October 2024. Analysts searched the force’s Storm incident database for terms including “UAP”, “unidentified aerial phenomena”, “extra-terrestrial”, “alien”, “light in the sky”, “UFO” and “spaceship”. They reported 21 relevant sightings between April 2014 and April 2024, after excluding incidents that clearly involved mental-health issues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset PoliceOctober 7, 2024 — UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police Your area UFO Sightings Freedom of Information...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2024</span></span></span>

### What happened after April 2024?

A later disclosure covering the whole of 2024 identified one additional incident, logged at 03:36 in July in Dorchester. The caller described blue and red things flying in the sky. Dorset Police said that its broader search initially retrieved many drone-related incidents, including anti-social behaviour, restricted-airspace activity and requests for the Force Drone Unit, but that those results were manually reviewed for relevance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2025-disclosures/ufo-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sightings &#124; Dorset PoliceFebruary 21, 2025 — UFO sightings &#124; Dorset Police Your area UFO sightings Freedom of Informatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 21, 2025</span></span></span>

## Recurring locations, shapes and light patterns

The dominant feature of the logs is not structured craft but **lights seen at night or under uncertain viewing conditions**. Fourteen of the 21 records were retrieved under variants of “light in the sky”. The descriptions include red, orange, bright, flashing, glowing and faint lights. Only a minority use stronger object language such as “spaceship”, “UFO” or “unidentified flying object”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset PoliceOctober 7, 2024 — UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police Your area UFO Sightings Freedom of Information...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2024</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. A caller saying that a light is “not an aeroplane” records an interpretation, not a demonstrated elimination of aircraft. Without information about direction, elevation, duration, movement, weather, visibility or nearby air traffic, even a sincere and accurate account cannot normally distinguish among aircraft lights, helicopters, drones, lanterns, stars, planets, meteors and other ordinary sources.

### The Poole triangles

The most visually dramatic entry dates from 14 October 2023. A Poole caller reported seeing approximately 18 black triangles, described in the log as “football size”, and believed them to be UFOs. ### Orange and red lights

Orange lights recur in Bournemouth in October 2020 and Weymouth in February 2023, where a caller reported three of them. Orange or reddish lights are common in civilian UFO reporting because many ordinary objects can acquire those colours. Aircraft navigation and anti-collision lights may appear red or flashing; lantern flames appear orange; drones can carry coloured LEDs; and atmospheric conditions can alter the colour and brightness of astronomical objects near the horizon. These are plausible categories, not proven explanations for particular Dorset calls.

The February 2023 Weymouth report is also a reminder that numbers alone do not identify structure. Three lights may belong to one triangular object, three independent objects or a conventional formation. The log contains no evidence that police established which interpretation was correct.

## What the dataset can and cannot prove

The disclosures prove that calls were entered into police systems and later retrieved by keyword searches. They do not prove that 21 anomalous craft were present, that every caller used the word “UFO”, or that all relevant reports were found.

The public table does not consistently provide:

* exact times;
* precise observation points or viewing directions;

* duration and apparent movement;
* weather and cloud conditions;
* photographs or video files;
* aircraft, helicopter or drone checks;

Without those details, the reports cannot be tested against flight records, satellite passes, bright planets, meteor activity or local events with much confidence. Even seemingly useful descriptions such as “red flashing”, “three orange lights” or “black triangles” remain ambiguous when distance and orientation are unknown.

A scientifically useful aerial observation would normally need simultaneous measurements from more than one sensor or location. Research proposals for systematic UAP study emphasise triangulated cameras, radar-derived range and speed, acoustic monitoring and environmental data precisely because a single visual report cannot securely establish an object’s size, distance or behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

## What the logs reveal about modern Dorset UFO reporting

Taken as a portfolio rather than as 21 isolated mysteries, the logs reveal three broad patterns.

First, **light reports greatly outnumber detailed craft descriptions**. The modern Dorset police record is principally a collection of brief observations of coloured or unusual lights. There are no released accounts comparable in detail to Dorset’s better-known historic sightings, and no case in this dataset includes publicly documented radar confirmation or physical evidence.

Second, **reporting is concentrated in populated and coastal parts of the police area**, particularly Poole, Bournemouth and Weymouth, but the sample is too small and inconsistently located to support a robust hotspot claim. Coastal horizons may provide long sightlines, but they also contain aircraft routes, vessels, harbour activity and distant lights that can be difficult to judge at night.

Third, **the records show uncertainty entering a governance system rather than being resolved by it**. Police logged what callers told them, filtered records for Freedom of Information purposes and, in at least one case, checked the scene without confirming the reported object. That process gives the reports an official documentary existence, but not official endorsement of the callers’ conclusions.

The fairest assessment is therefore that Dorset Police’s UFO logs preserve genuine reports of puzzling experiences, including a few vivid claims, but provide no verified example of an extraordinary craft. Their chief historical value lies in showing what residents noticed, how they described it and how limited the surviving official information usually is.
