Reported by forestry worker Robert “Bob” Taylor, the incident became one of Scotland’s most discussed UFO cases because it combined a close-range witness account, alleged physical traces, and a police investigation into what was initially treated as a possible assault. ## The Dechmont Woods encounter: West Lothian’s defining UFO case

### What Robert Taylor reported in 1979

On the morning of 9 November 1979, Robert Taylor, a 61-year-old forestry worker, was carrying out routine work in Dechmont Woods near Livingston. Taylor later said he encountered a large dark, dome-shaped object hovering close to the ground in a clearing. According to his account, two smaller spherical objects with projections moved towards him, causing him to lose consciousness. When he recovered, he said the object had disappeared and that his trousers had been torn.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.westlothian.gov.uk/media/26988/Dechmont-Law-UFO-info/pdf/Dechmont_Law_UFO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: westlothian.gov.uk">[West Lothian Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">westlothian.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">West Lothian Council Top secret</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">West Lothian CouncilTop secretNovember 8, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 8, 2025</span></span></span>

The case attracted attention because Taylor was not a typical attention-seeker in the eyes of those who knew him. Supporters of the account have emphasised his reputation as a reliable local man and the fact that he reported the incident through ordinary channels rather than seeking publicity immediately. The incident was also unusual because it led to police involvement: officers investigated the circumstances as they would for a possible physical assault, examining the scene and the damage to Taylor’s clothing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.westlothian.gov.uk/dechmontlaw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: westlothian.gov.uk">[West Lothian Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">westlothian.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">West Lothian Council Dechmont Law</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">West Lothian CouncilDechmont Law - West Lothian Council...</span></span></span>

For UFO researchers, the strongest part of the case has traditionally been the combination of witness testimony and alleged physical evidence. The reported marks at the site and the damage to Taylor’s trousers made the incident stand apart from many UFO reports that rely only on a distant observation or a brief sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ghosttheory.com/2015/09/14/the-dechmont-woods-encounter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ghosttheory.com">[ghosttheory.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ghosttheory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Dechmont Woods Encounter – Ghost Theory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 14, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2015</span></span></span>

### Why the case remains controversial

The Dechmont Woods encounter is not considered proof of an extraterrestrial craft. The evidence is limited largely to Taylor’s testimony, the physical scene as interpreted after the event, and later accounts from investigators and researchers. No object was recovered, no independent witnesses observed the alleged craft, and no radar or aviation records have established an unusual aircraft presence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.westlothian.gov.uk/dechmontlaw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: westlothian.gov.uk">[West Lothian Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">westlothian.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">West Lothian Council Dechmont Law</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">West Lothian CouncilDechmont Law - West Lothian Council...</span></span></span>

Several alternative explanations have been suggested. Some sceptical investigators have explored possibilities involving misperception, unusual natural phenomena, or an ordinary object being interpreted in extraordinary circumstances. One proposed explanation involved an atmospheric mirage involving bright planets, although this interpretation has also been criticised as incomplete by some UFO researchers because it does not easily account for every detail of Taylor’s account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/dechmontwoods79.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ufo - UFOs at close sight: Dechmont Woods, Scotland, November 9, 1979...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 1979</span></span></span>

Another challenge is that the physical evidence has never produced a clear answer. Investigators have disagreed about what caused the marks reported at the scene, with some arguing they indicated a mechanical object while others have looked for more conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ghosttheory.com/2015/09/14/the-dechmont-woods-encounter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ghosttheory.com">[ghosttheory.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ghosttheory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Dechmont Woods Encounter – Ghost Theory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 14, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2015</span></span></span>

The most balanced assessment is that the Dechmont Woods case is a genuine historical mystery: a sincere witness reported an unusual experience, authorities investigated it, and some details remain difficult to explain. At the same time, the available evidence does not demonstrate that the event involved a spacecraft or non-human technology.

## West Lothian’s wider UFO record

### Local sightings and newspaper reports

Compared with areas sometimes described as Scottish UFO “hotspots”, West Lothian has a smaller and less consistent record. Newspaper archives contain reports of UFO-related stories from places including Bathgate, Linlithgow and other parts of the county, particularly during the 1970s and later decades. These reports include sightings of unusual lights or objects, but most do not have the level of documentation associated with the Dechmont Woods case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results/1950-01-01/1999-12-31?basicsearch=ufo&amp;county=west+lothian%2C+scotland&amp;mostspecificlocation=lothian%2C+scotland&amp;region=lothian%2C+scotland&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false&amp;somesearch=ufo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Newspaper ArchiveResults for &#x27;ufo&#x27; &#124; Between 1st Jan 1950 and 31st Dec 1999 &#124; West Lothian, Scotland &#124; Location &#124; British Newspap...</span></span></span>

A notable example from local reporting is the wider interest in unusual aerial sightings during the 1970s, when public fascination with UFOs was widespread across Britain. Many reports from this period reflected a mixture of possible aircraft sightings, astronomical objects, atmospheric effects and genuinely unexplained observations. A report being labelled “unidentified” means only that an observer or investigator did not establish a definite explanation; it does not mean the object was proven unusual in origin.

### Aviation and military context

West Lothian’s location between Edinburgh and central Scotland places it beneath active air routes and within a region where aircraft activity is common. This matters because many UFO reports nationally have later been linked to aircraft lights, helicopters, satellites, meteors, balloons or other ordinary causes.

The Ministry of Defence historically collected UFO reports as part of its air defence responsibilities, but the existence of a report did not mean officials accepted an extraordinary explanation. UK government UFO files released through public archives show that the MoD recorded and assessed reports primarily to consider possible defence implications, not to confirm extraterrestrial claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1998-12-17/debates/7ec2271e-4a74-46e4-a38e-1224a002c04a/UnidentifiedFlyingObjects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Unidentified Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified Flying Objects - Hansard - UK ParliamentDecember 17, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 17, 1998</span></span></span>

## How the Dechmont case changed West Lothian’s UFO reputation

The lasting importance of West Lothian in UFO history comes from how unusual the Dechmont incident was compared with typical sightings. Most UFO reports involve distant lights or objects seen briefly in the sky. Taylor’s account instead described a close encounter at ground level involving alleged interaction with the witness and a physical aftermath.

This helped make Dechmont Woods a recognised location among UFO researchers and visitors interested in unusual history. West Lothian Council has acknowledged the site’s cultural significance by providing information about the incident at Dechmont Law, while also presenting it as a local curiosity rather than as an established fact about alien activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.westlothian.gov.uk/dechmontlaw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: westlothian.gov.uk">[West Lothian Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">westlothian.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">West Lothian Council Dechmont Law</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">West Lothian CouncilDechmont Law - West Lothian Council...</span></span></span>

The case also illustrates a wider issue in UFO [investigations]({{ 'investigations-8529f5/' | relative_url }}): unusual reports can remain unresolved without requiring either complete acceptance or complete dismissal. A witness can be regarded as sincere while the cause of the experience remains uncertain.
