## Introduction

The Hersham fireball sighting is one of [Surrey]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-surreys-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s better-documented modern UFO reports because it was recorded in the UK Ministry of Defence (MoD) UFO files rather than existing only as a local anecdote. The report describes a large orange fireball seen over Hersham, Surrey, on 24 August 2009 at around 21:45, with the object reportedly visible for about 35 seconds, producing no sound and gradually reducing in size until it disappeared. The case remains an unidentified report in the official record, but it was never confirmed as an unusual craft. The available evidence fits several ordinary possibilities, particularly a bright meteor or another atmospheric object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo reports in the uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 4, 2007 — 4 Dec 2007 — Ministry of Defence. ## The 2009 Hersham report and witness account

The Hersham incident was logged in the MoD’s 2009 UFO report database as a Surrey sighting. The official entry records a “large orange fireball” that lasted approximately 35 seconds. It notes that there was no noise from the object and that its apparent size decreased over the course of the observation until it was no longer visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7582c440f0b6397f35efcb/ufo_report_2009.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report 2009</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Reports 2009 for MoD website-Edited12 Jan 2009 — Hersham Surrey A large orange fireball burning for 35 seconds. The description is typical of a category of UFO reports involving bright lights or glowing objects rather than structured craft. A fireball-like appearance can be striking to an observer because bright meteors can appear to move across a large section of sky, change brightness, fragment, or seem to slow as they approach the horizon. Human perception also makes judging distance, size and speed difficult when there is no fixed reference point, especially at night.

Hersham’s location adds some relevant context. The area sits within the busy south-western part of the London commuter belt, where aircraft movements, high-altitude objects and atmospheric phenomena are regularly visible. However, the MoD entry itself does not identify an aircraft, meteor event or other definite source, leaving the report classified only as an unexplained sighting rather than a solved case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7582c440f0b6397f35efcb/ufo_report_2009.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report 2009</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Reports 2009 for MoD website-Edited12 Jan 2009 — Hersham Surrey A large orange fireball burning for 35 seconds. ## How government files recorded the sighting

The Hersham report appeared in the final period of the MoD’s public UFO reporting system. The department collected reports from members of the public, recording details such as date, time, location and a short description of what witnesses believed they had seen. These files were later released through government publications covering UK UFO reports from 1997 to 2009.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo reports in the uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 4, 2007 — 4 Dec 2007 — Ministry of Defence. The purpose of the MoD process was primarily related to defence and aviation awareness, not a scientific search for extraterrestrial activity. A report being included in the archive meant that it had been submitted and recorded; it did not mean officials had verified the witness interpretation. This distinction is particularly important in cases such as Hersham, where the strongest evidence is the existence of the report itself rather than independent confirmation of the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo reports in the uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 4, 2007 — 4 Dec 2007 — Ministry of Defence. The release of the 2009 files also placed Hersham among many other UK sightings reported in the same period. The archive contains a wide variety of observations, including unusual lights, shapes and objects later considered likely to have conventional explanations. The presence of multiple reports in a database shows patterns of public observation but does not by itself demonstrate that the events share a common cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ufo-reports-in-the-uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo reports in the uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 4, 2007 — 4 Dec 2007 — Ministry of Defence. ## Possible fireball and aircraft explanations

The most straightforward explanation considered for the Hersham sighting is a natural fireball event. In astronomy, a fireball is an especially bright meteor caused by a meteoroid entering Earth’s atmosphere. Such events can appear as intense orange, yellow or white lights and may last longer than ordinary shooting stars, depending on their trajectory and brightness. Fireball observation networks use witness reports because multiple observations can help estimate a meteor’s path and behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.imo.net/observations/fireballs/fireball-report-program/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imo.net">[International Meteor Organization]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Meteor Organization Fireball Program &#124; IMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Meteor Organization Fireball Program &#124; IMO</span></span></span>

The Hersham description contains several features that are compatible with a fireball interpretation: a bright orange appearance, a short duration, silent operation and a gradual disappearance. These features do not prove that the object was a meteor, because the report lacks independent measurements such as video, radar tracking or observations from multiple locations. However, they make a natural explanation plausible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7582c440f0b6397f35efcb/ufo_report_2009.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo report 2009</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Reports 2009 for MoD website-Edited12 Jan 2009 — Hersham Surrey A large orange fireball burning for 35 seconds. Aircraft and other human-made objects are another possible source of mistaken identification in UFO reports. Lights seen from the ground can appear unusual when viewed at unfamiliar angles, through haze or without visible navigation references. The Hersham record does not provide enough detail to match the object confidently to an aircraft, but aviation remains part of the normal range of explanations considered for sightings of this kind.
