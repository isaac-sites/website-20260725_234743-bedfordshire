## Introduction

Welshpool’s reputation for unusual aerial reports rests on a small number of fragmentary records rather than a well-documented UFO “flap”. The most detailed story is a retrospective account of three coloured lights seen in a triangular formation in November 1992. A later police disclosure mentioned a Welshpool caller reporting a UFO “dropping in the distance”, but supplied too little information to establish the date, duration, direction or likely cause. Neither case is backed by photographs, radar data or a surviving contemporary investigation.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Montgomeryshire_1814c4_welshpool_border_rep_a19e7b-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Welshpool Reports" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The border location helps explain why the story appears larger than the evidence. Welshpool lies on transport and aviation corridors connecting mid Wales with Shropshire, and reports are variously filed under Welshpool, [Montgomeryshire]({{ 'what-really-flew-over-montgomeryshire/' | relative_url }}), Powys, the Welsh border or the wider Dyfed-Powys Police area. Nearby military flying and better-known Shropshire incidents add useful context, but they should not be treated as confirmation that the same object or phenomenon was seen over Welshpool.

## The 1992 triangle and coloured lights

The principal Welshpool case is dated to approximately 6.15 pm on 19 November 1992. According to an account later given by Michael Hopkins, he was driving south through Welshpool after attending a meeting at Valle Crucis Abbey near Llangollen. The present A483 bypass had not yet diverted traffic around the town, so his route took him through the built-up area. He recalled five or six cars ahead slowing almost to a halt, apparently without an obstruction on the road.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/return-of-the-triangles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swansea Ufo Network BLACK TRIANGLE DATABASE &#124; Swansea Ufo Network</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo Network BLACK TRIANGLE DATABASE &#124; Swansea Ufo Network</span></span></span>

Hopkins said he looked towards the left and saw red, white and blue lights forming a triangle above trees. The formation appeared almost stationary, then descended with a “falling leaf” motion and rose again in two distinct stages. He remembered this sequence repeating several times. Looking in the opposite direction, he then noticed a separate orange light moving slowly towards the north-east. After watching it for about ten seconds, he looked back and found that the triangular formation had disappeared. The reported observation point was just south of Welshpool.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/return-of-the-triangles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swansea Ufo Network BLACK TRIANGLE DATABASE &#124; Swansea Ufo Network</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo Network BLACK TRIANGLE DATABASE &#124; Swansea Ufo Network</span></span></span>

This is more interesting than a simple report of a single bright point because the witness described colour, geometry and repeated movement. The slowing traffic also raises the possibility that other motorists noticed something. However, those motorists are not identified as witnesses, and there are no known statements from them. It cannot be assumed that they slowed because of the lights rather than congestion, a junction, an animal, road conditions or some other ordinary cause.

The largest weakness is the reporting history. The account is attributed directly to Hopkins in 2018, around 26 years after the alleged sighting. The available entry does not cite a contemporary diary, newspaper report, police call, Ministry of Defence form or aviation record from November 1992.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/return-of-the-triangles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swansea Ufo Network BLACK TRIANGLE DATABASE &#124; Swansea Ufo Network</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo Network BLACK TRIANGLE DATABASE &#124; Swansea Ufo Network</span></span></span> Memory can preserve the central impression of an event while becoming less reliable about exact timings, colours, directions and sequences over such a long interval.

There is also an important distinction between **three lights forming a triangle** and a **solid triangular craft**. The witness account describes a formation of lights but does not clearly state that a dark body, fixed edges or an object blocking the stars was visible between them. Separate aircraft viewed from an angle, an aircraft changing attitude, lights seen through branches, or a distant object whose motion was distorted by the observer’s own movement could all create apparent geometry. The second orange light may have been connected, but the account provides no evidence that it was.

The “falling leaf” movement is the case’s hardest feature to interpret. Genuine vertical oscillation would be unusual for a conventional fixed-wing aircraft, but apparent rising and falling can result from changing brightness, movement behind uneven trees, atmospheric distortion or an observer repeatedly losing and regaining visual reference. Without a measured bearing, elevation, weather record or fixed foreground comparison, the reported motion cannot be reconstructed.

The fairest classification is therefore **unresolved but weakly evidenced**. The testimony describes something the witness could not identify, but the long delay, absence of corroborating witnesses and lack of contemporary records prevent stronger conclusions.

## What the police records actually show


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Welshpool’s second widely repeated case comes from information attributed to Dyfed-Powys Police and reported by the *Shropshire Star* in July 2014. The newspaper said a Welshpool caller had reported seeing a UFO “dropping in the distance”. It also stated that the force had received 27 UFO reports since 2002 across its entire policing area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirestar.com/news/2014/07/09/police-confirm-surge-of-reported-ufo-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirestar.com">[Shropshire Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirestar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Star Police confirm surge of reported UFO sightings &#124; Shropshire Star</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire Star Police confirm surge of reported UFO sightings &#124; Shropshire Star</span></span></span>

</div>

That wording is often treated as if it were a detailed police case file. It is not. The published report gives no exact date for the Welshpool call, no time, no observation point and no description of shape, colour or duration. It does not say whether officers attended, whether the caller remained on the telephone, whether another witness was located, or whether the object was compared with aircraft, meteors, lanterns or astronomical bodies. “Dropping in the distance” might describe a fast meteor, an aircraft descending towards a runway, a flare, a lantern losing altitude or simply a light disappearing behind terrain.

The figure of 27 reports also requires care. It covered the whole Dyfed-Powys Police jurisdiction, not Welshpool or historic Montgomeryshire alone. That force area extends far beyond the town and included calls ranging from distant lights to alleged alien encounters and paranormal complaints. The newspaper’s examples included Llanybydder, Aberystwyth and Llandysul, demonstrating how geographically and evidentially mixed the material was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirestar.com/news/2014/07/09/police-confirm-surge-of-reported-ufo-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirestar.com">[Shropshire Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirestar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Star Police confirm surge of reported UFO sightings &#124; Shropshire Star</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire Star Police confirm surge of reported UFO sightings &#124; Shropshire Star</span></span></span> Calling Welshpool a hotspot on the strength of a force-wide total therefore overstates the local pattern.

The police themselves offered only a general statement that calls received appropriate action and enquiries where necessary. That indicates incident handling, not validation of extraordinary claims. A police log proves that somebody contacted the force and that the call was recorded. It does not prove that the reported object was physically unusual, that officers saw it, or that an investigation found no conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirestar.com/news/2014/07/09/police-confirm-surge-of-reported-ufo-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirestar.com">[Shropshire Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirestar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Star Police confirm surge of reported UFO sightings &#124; Shropshire Star</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire Star Police confirm surge of reported UFO sightings &#124; Shropshire Star</span></span></span>

More recent Freedom of Information responses show why reconstructing the record is difficult. Dyfed-Powys Police stated in 2023 that it could not simply search its systems for UFO-related terms and would have to inspect incidents individually, including records categorised broadly as suspicious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dyfed-powys.police.uk/foi-ai/dyfed-powys-police/disclosure-2023/january/ufo-uap-sightings-9972022/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dyfed-powys.police.uk">[Dyfed-Powys Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dyfed-powys.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo uap sightings 9972022</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO/ UAP sightings 997/2022 &#124; Dyfed-Powys Police...</span></span></span> In a 2025 request concerning Powys, the force identified 106 potentially relevant records from 2020 to 2024 but refused a full breakdown because each would require manual review. It also warned that the recording systems meant released information might not be fully accurate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dyfed-powys.police.uk/foi-ai/dyfed-powys-police/disclosure-2025/may/ufo-and-alien-sightings-3572025/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dyfed-powys.police.uk">[Dyfed-Powys Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dyfed-powys.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo and alien sightings 3572025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO and alien sightings 357/2025 &#124; Dyfed-Powys Police...</span></span></span>

Those 106 records should not be read as 106 confirmed UFO sightings. They are records requiring inspection to determine whether they are relevant to search terms such as UFOs or aliens. Some may concern misidentifications, mental-health-related calls, jokes, drones, suspicious aircraft or unrelated uses of a keyword. The response demonstrates the limitations of police databases rather than a sudden concentration of unexplained craft over Powys.

The Welshpool log consequently remains a useful archival trace but a poor-quality sighting record. Its value is that it confirms at least one local caller used UFO language when contacting the police. Its weakness is that virtually all of the information needed to assess the observation is absent from the published version.

## Why Powys and Shropshire reports become mixed

Welshpool sits close enough to the English border for lights seen over one jurisdiction to be reported from another. The town is linked eastwards to Shrewsbury by the A458 and north-eastwards through the border country by the A483. A witness may be standing in Wales while looking towards an object over Shropshire, or travelling between the two when the observation begins. Newspaper circulation also crosses the boundary, particularly through Shropshire-based regional coverage.

This matters because articles about the Welshpool police call often place it alongside the far more elaborate Cosford incident of 30–31 March 1993. During that event, reports were made across western Britain, and witnesses associated with RAF Cosford and RAF Shawbury described unusual lights or a large triangular form. The existence of a substantial Ministry of Defence file covering the reports is confirmed by The National Archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/record?catid=-5839206&amp;catln=7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery National ArchivesDigital copy of DEFE 24/2086: UFO reports sightings, 30/31 March 1993; with redactions &#124; The National Archives...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1993</span></span></span>

Cosford occurred only four months after the claimed Welshpool sighting, involved triangular imagery and took place within the wider border aviation region. That makes it a natural comparison, but not evidence of continuity. No available source connects the 19 November 1992 Welshpool lights with the March 1993 reports. The witnesses, timings, trajectories and documentation are separate. Similar descriptive language can arise because aircraft navigation lights commonly form apparent triangles, particularly when viewed head-on or at an oblique angle.

Military activity is a credible part of the background. RAF Shawbury states that Low Flying Area 9 covers Shropshire and the borders of neighbouring counties. It is used for intensive helicopter training, including periodic night flying, and crews may operate at very low altitude in rural areas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/our-organisation/stations/raf-shawbury/flying-info/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Air ForceFlying Info &#124; RAF Shawbury &#124; Royal Air ForceFlying Info &#124; RAF Shawbury &#124; Royal Air Force All stations RAF Shawbury Overvie...</span></span></span> This does not explain the Welshpool cases automatically: the 1992 report contains no engine noise or direct helicopter identification, and the date would need checking against historical flying records. ## Does Welshpool show a genuine cluster?

There is a modest cluster of **reports associated with the name Welshpool**, but not a demonstrated cluster of linked phenomena. The 1992 testimony concerns a triangular arrangement of coloured lights and an orange light. The police record concerns an unspecified object apparently descending in the distance. Later summaries sometimes combine these with Powys-wide statistics, mid-Wales videos and Shropshire triangle stories, producing the impression of repeated sightings over one small area.

The evidence does not support that impression. The two core Welshpool cases are separated by an uncertain number of years, were recorded through different channels and contain too little directional or temporal information to compare. There is no documented sequence of reports on the same night, no repeated observation point, no matching photographs and no police, radar or aviation record tying them together.

Welshpool nevertheless matters within Montgomeryshire’s UFO history because it demonstrates how local legends are assembled. A memorable retrospective account, a short phrase extracted from a police log and nearby incidents from another county can become a regional narrative even when the original records remain disconnected. The border does not prevent genuine patterns from crossing jurisdictions, but it makes careful indexing essential.

On the available evidence, the 1992 report deserves preservation as an unusual witness account, while the police call should be treated as a minimal incident entry rather than an investigated case. Both remain unidentified in the everyday sense that no secure explanation is recorded. Neither provides persuasive evidence of an exotic craft, and later reporting has increased their visibility more than it has strengthened their evidential basis.
