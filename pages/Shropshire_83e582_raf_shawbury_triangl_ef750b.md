## Introduction

The RAF Shawbury black triangle sighting of 31 March 1993 is one of the most discussed UFO reports connected with [Shropshire]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-shropshires/' | relative_url }}), largely because the central witness was a serving RAF meteorological officer and because the event occurred close to military airfields. The evidence is unusual in scale but not conclusive: there are witness statements, Ministry of Defence (MoD) records and a formal review, yet no publicly released evidence proving that the object was an unknown aircraft or anything beyond an unidentified observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1998-07-15/debates/4c752226-9b55-4e3d-b833-4a03f3b5a48b/UnidentifiedFlyingObjects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Unidentified Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified Flying Objects - Hansard - UK ParliamentJuly 15, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 1998</span></span></span>

The case matters because it sits at the intersection of several factors that make aerial reports more likely to attract attention: a military environment, trained observers, multiple reports across the wider region and descriptions of a large triangular object apparently moving in ways witnesses found difficult to explain. At the same time, later investigations separated parts of the wider 1993 event that could be explained, such as high-altitude sightings linked to a rocket re-entry, from the more specific low-level RAF Shawbury report that remained unresolved in public records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives Media]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Media The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives MediaThe Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National ArchivesMay 23, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2019</span></span></span>

## The 1993 sighting and witness accounts

The RAF Shawbury report formed part of a wider series of sightings during the night of 30–31 March 1993, involving reports around RAF Cosford, RAF Shawbury and other locations in the Midlands and beyond. The Shawbury account is usually attributed to the station’s meteorological officer, Wayne Elliott, who reported seeing a large triangular or wedge-shaped object in the early hours of 31 March. Contemporary descriptions collected by UFO researchers and later discussed in connection with MoD files describe an object apparently travelling slowly, with a bright beam directed towards the ground and a low-frequency sound reported by the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirelive.com/features/2010/11/13/phil-hoyle-shropshire-ufo-casebook-part-2-the-triangle-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirelive.com">[Shropshire Live]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirelive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Live Phil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire LivePhil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle MysteryNovember 13, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2010</span></span></span>

The strongest element of the case is not simply that one person reported an unusual object, but that the witness was a trained RAF observer operating in a location where aircraft activity was normal. Supporters of the case argue that this makes the report more significant than a casual sighting from an unknown observer. The account also became linked with the RAF Cosford reports from the same night, where RAF personnel described seeing a large triangular object passing over the base area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://realufo.org/stories/cosford/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: realufo.org">[realufo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">realufo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1993 — UK National Archives · realufo.orgMarch 30, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 1993</span></span></span>

However, witness expertise does not automatically identify an object. Even trained observers can misjudge distance, altitude, size and speed when viewing unusual lights or shapes at night. The MoD’s later position was that the reports were examined but no firm conclusion about the nature of the object was reached.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1996-07-24/debates/4648c095-a881-46c9-b8e2-ac917b883ad2/UnidentifiedCraft" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Unidentified Craft</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified Craft - Hansard - UK Parliament...</span></span></span>

The most frequently repeated features of the Shawbury account include:

* **A large triangular shape:** The object was described as a dark, solid-looking triangle or wedge rather than a conventional aircraft silhouette.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirelive.com/features/2010/11/13/phil-hoyle-shropshire-ufo-casebook-part-2-the-triangle-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirelive.com">[Shropshire Live]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirelive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Live Phil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire LivePhil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle MysteryNovember 13, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2010</span></span></span>
* **A ground-directed light:** The reported beam sweeping across the ground is one of the details that made the case stand out from ordinary distant-light sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirelive.com/features/2010/11/13/phil-hoyle-shropshire-ufo-casebook-part-2-the-triangle-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirelive.com">[Shropshire Live]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirelive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Live Phil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire LivePhil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle MysteryNovember 13, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2010</span></span></span>
* **Unusual movement:** Some accounts describe apparent acceleration or movement that seemed inconsistent with known aircraft, although these estimates depended on visual judgement rather than instrument measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shropshirelive.com/features/2010/11/13/phil-hoyle-shropshire-ufo-casebook-part-2-the-triangle-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shropshirelive.com">[Shropshire Live]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shropshirelive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shropshire Live Phil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shropshire LivePhil Hoyle Shropshire UFO Casebook Part 2 The Triangle MysteryNovember 13, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2010</span></span></span>
* **A military setting:** The proximity of RAF Shawbury and RAF Cosford gave the report greater public interest and encouraged speculation about experimental aircraft or defence-related activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Military connections and possible explanations

RAF Shawbury’s role as a military aviation site is central to why the report became prominent. Sightings near airfields naturally attract greater scrutiny because witnesses and investigators consider possibilities ranging from routine aircraft activity to classified technology. The presence of RAF personnel also meant the report entered official channels rather than remaining only a local story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1998-07-15/debates/4c752226-9b55-4e3d-b833-4a03f3b5a48b/UnidentifiedFlyingObjects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Unidentified Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified Flying Objects - Hansard - UK ParliamentJuly 15, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 1998</span></span></span>

The MoD investigated the broader Cosford–Shawbury incident through its UFO reporting system. Parliamentary records later stated that the department was aware of a report from two military personnel concerning the 31 March 1993 event, that the facts were examined, and that no firm conclusion was drawn about what had been seen. The incident was not assessed as being of defence significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1998-07-15/debates/4c752226-9b55-4e3d-b833-4a03f3b5a48b/UnidentifiedFlyingObjects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Unidentified Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified Flying Objects - Hansard - UK ParliamentJuly 15, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 1998</span></span></span>

Several explanations have been considered:

**Rocket re-entry explanation**

One part of the wider March 1993 sighting wave was linked to the re-entry of a Russian satellite rocket booster. The National Archives notes that some reports from that night, particularly high-altitude observations, were ultimately explained through this type of event. This explanation helped account for some sightings but did not settle every report associated with the RAF Shawbury and RAF Cosford observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/cold-war-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives Media]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Media The Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives MediaThe Cold War and UFOs &#124; The National ArchivesMay 23, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2019</span></span></span>

**Conventional aircraft or aviation activity**

Sceptical interpretations point out that night-time observations near military airspace can be difficult to judge. Aircraft seen from unusual angles can appear triangular because of their lighting arrangement, and estimating size or speed without reference points is particularly unreliable. ## Why the RAF Shawbury case remains significant in Shropshire UFO history

The RAF Shawbury black triangle report remains one of Shropshire’s most notable UFO stories because it combines elements rarely found together in local sightings: a named military location, a professional witness, official attention and a description that continues to resist a single accepted explanation. Its importance lies less in proving what the object was and more in showing how unusual aerial reports are assessed when they involve credible witnesses but limited physical evidence.

The case also illustrates a recurring challenge in UFO investigations. An incident can be genuine as a report — meaning people really saw and recorded something they could not identify — while the explanation remains uncertain. The RAF Shawbury sighting therefore occupies a middle ground: more substantial than an unsupported rumour, but not supported by the evidence needed to confirm a revolutionary explanation.
