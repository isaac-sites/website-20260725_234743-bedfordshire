## Introduction

[County Down]({{ 'what-really-flew-over-county-down/' | relative_url }})’s police UFO logs prove that people reported unusual things in the sky; they do not prove that unexplained craft were present. The clearest recent entry concerns a Bangor caller who described a bright, colour-changing light recorded on a phone and mentioned an earlier experience near Cloghy on the Ards Peninsula. The Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) preserved the account, but the released log contains no radar check, original video, precise direction, duration, weather conditions or independent witness statement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psni.police.uk/foi-disclosure-log/sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psni.police.uk">[Police Service of Northern Ireland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psni.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Police Service of Northern IrelandSightings &#124; PSNIJune 23, 2025 — Sightings &#124; PSNI Home / FOI Disclosure Log / Sightings Sightings June 2...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 23, 2025</span></span></span>

That distinction is the key to reading police records responsibly. An incident log is evidence that a report was made, usually in the caller’s own terms. Unless officers or another competent body subsequently collect and test further information, it is not an official finding that the reported object was genuinely unidentifiable. In the available County Down cases, the logs are historically useful but scientifically weak.

## The Bangor and Cloghy reports

The most detailed recent County Down entry was recorded by the PSNI on 11 December 2024. The caller said that, from her bedroom window in Bangor, she had seen what she thought was a UFO during the previous April at about 11pm. According to the log, the object first resembled a very bright star, disappeared, reappeared in red and green, and then moved across the sky “like a plane”. She said that she had recorded it on her phone. She also recalled seeing something with friends near Cloghy years earlier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psni.police.uk/foi-disclosure-log/sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psni.police.uk">[Police Service of Northern Ireland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psni.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Police Service of Northern IrelandSightings &#124; PSNIJune 23, 2025 — Sightings &#124; PSNI Home / FOI Disclosure Log / Sightings Sightings June 2...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 23, 2025</span></span></span>

The report contains several useful facts: an approximate month and time, an observing location, changes in colour and brightness, apparent motion, and the claimed existence of a recording. It also contains details that favour ordinary explanations. Red and green lights are characteristic of aircraft position lighting, while aircraft operating at night must carry navigation or position lights and an anti-collision light system. An approaching aircraft may initially resemble a nearly stationary bright point before its sideways movement becomes apparent as its direction relative to the observer changes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/air-operations-pre-30-october-2025/Content/Document%20Structure/06%20NCC/2%20Regs/16350_NCCIDEA115_Operating_lights.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk">[Regulatory Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Regulatory LibraryNCC.IDE.A.115 Operating lightsNCC.IDE.A.115 Operating lights You are here: Annex VI (Part NCC) &gt; SUBPART D: INSTRUMENTS...</span></span></span>

That does not establish that the Bangor object was an aircraft. The problem is that the log provides too little information to test the possibility. It does not say which compass direction the caller faced, how high the light appeared, how long it remained visible, whether it passed behind buildings, whether sound was delayed, or whether flights were operating on a matching route. Without those details, “aircraft” is plausible rather than demonstrated.

The Cloghy reference is weaker still. It is a brief recollection within a later telephone call, with no date, time, description or original incident number in the released material. It cannot presently be treated as an independent documented case. At most, it shows that the Bangor caller interpreted the newer sighting in the light of an earlier experience shared with friends.

Other County Down records show the same limitations. During 2021, police received a report of a “space ship and flashing lights” around Downpatrick and another of a dome-shaped object with eight lights near Saintfield. The PSNI stated that it conducted no investigations into that year’s UFO-related calls. The entries therefore preserve the language used by callers but supply no tested identification, supporting instrument data or official conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/northern-ireland-ufo-nick-pope-police-united-states-b1982455.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.co.uk">[The Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IndependentReports of unexplained sightings in skies above Northern Ireland increase &#124; The IndependentDecember 27, 2021 — Reports of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 27, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why an incident log is not a scientific finding

Police call records are created for operational purposes. A call handler’s first task is to understand what is being reported, whether anyone is in danger and whether police action is required. College of Policing guidance describes such an initial account as information used to guide immediate decisions and any later investigation; it also warns that potentially important details may be lost when they are not elicited at this early stage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.college.police.uk/guidance/obtaining-initial-accounts/introduction-initial-accounts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: college.police.uk">[College of Policing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">college.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Obtaining a first account is not always considered part of the investigation, but if essential information is missed or not drawn out at...</span></span></span>

A UFO log may consequently reproduce a caller’s interpretation — “UFO”, “orb” or “spaceship” — without endorsing it. The PSNI’s disclosure is a list of recorded reports, not a catalogue of verified objects. The difference is especially important when journalists count annual “sightings”. A rise from four reports in 2019 to eight in 2021 describes changes in reporting to police, not a measured increase in unusual aerial objects. The 2021 total included widely different experiences, from lights in the sky to claims involving aliens, and none was investigated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/northern-ireland-ufo-nick-pope-police-united-states-b1982455.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.co.uk">[The Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IndependentReports of unexplained sightings in skies above Northern Ireland increase &#124; The IndependentDecember 27, 2021 — Reports of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 27, 2021</span></span></span>

The categories used to retrieve records can distort the picture further. The PSNI has searched terms including UFO, unidentified aerial phenomena, lights in the sky, aliens and extra-terrestrial. That is useful for locating possible entries, but it joins together observations that may have entirely different causes and levels of credibility. A brief report of a distant light, a camera anomaly and a claim made in a medical or custodial setting should not be counted as equivalent pieces of evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/northern-ireland-ufo-nick-pope-police-united-states-b1982455.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.co.uk">[The Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IndependentReports of unexplained sightings in skies above Northern Ireland increase &#124; The IndependentDecember 27, 2021 — Reports of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 27, 2021</span></span></span>

Freedom of Information disclosures also reflect how searchable the police database happens to be. In 2025 the PSNI refused broader requests for a decade of UFO and unidentified-phenomena material because retrieving and reviewing the records would require a manual trawl exceeding the statutory cost limit. The force said that relevant information was held electronically but not in a form that allowed straightforward extraction. This means the published lists should not automatically be treated as a complete scientific dataset of everything seen over Northern Ireland.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psni.police.uk/foi-disclosure-log/reports-unidentified-phenomena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psni.police.uk">[Police Service of Northern Ireland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psni.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reports unidentified phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">reports unidentified phenomena</span></span></span>

Even a police record that may later form part of an investigation is not automatically proof of the claim it contains. UK criminal-justice guidance distinguishes between information recorded during an inquiry and material ultimately judged to have evidential significance. An incident account can be valuable because it is contemporaneous, but its reliability still depends on corroboration, consistency and further examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cps.gov.uk/prosecution-guidance/dg6-desktop-guide" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cps.gov.uk">[Crown Prosecution Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cps.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Crown Prosecution Service DG6 Desktop Guide &#124; The Crown Prosecution Service</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Crown Prosecution Service DG6 Desktop Guide &#124; The Crown Prosecution Service</span></span></span>

## Aircraft lights, phone effects and missing data

The Bangor description illustrates why isolated night-time lights are difficult to identify. Human observers generally lack reference points for judging the distance and size of a light against a dark sky. A bright point may appear to hover when moving towards the observer, then seem to accelerate once its path becomes lateral. Changes in atmospheric clarity, thin cloud or the object’s orientation can also alter its brightness.

The reported transition from white to red and green is compatible with seeing different aircraft lights as the viewing angle changed. It could also reflect atmospheric effects or the way a phone processed a small, overexposed light source. Northern Ireland’s coastal viewpoints add further ambiguity because observers around Bangor and the Ards Peninsula can see lights associated with aircraft, vessels and distant shorelines across a broad, dark horizon.

A phone recording would potentially improve the case, but only if the original file were examined. Smartphone footage of a distant point usually lacks enough detail to show an object’s structure. Digital zoom enlarges pixels rather than recovering missing information, and automatic focus or exposure adjustments can make a light pulse, blur, change apparent size or vanish temporarily. Internal reflections within a lens can also generate coloured spots or “ghost” images, particularly when a strong light source is in or just outside the frame. Optical research describes smartphone flare as an artefact caused by light reflecting within the lens system, producing bright secondary shapes that do not correspond to separate objects in the scene.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15046" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

The released PSNI log does not state whether police viewed the Bangor recording, retained a copy or checked its metadata. Nor does it record the make of phone, zoom level, orientation, original resolution or whether the light’s movement matched camera shake. Saying that a recording exists is therefore not equivalent to having analysed photographic evidence.

The same evidential weakness affected Britain’s much larger Ministry of Defence UFO archive. The Defence Intelligence study commonly known as Project Condign noted that reports frequently contained approximate directions and imprecise numerical estimates, while the files held few useful photographs and no adequate body of instrumented measurements. It concluded that reports often arose when witnesses encountered familiar objects or phenomena in unfamiliar circumstances. Although that study concerned national defence files rather than the PSNI’s County Down logs, its warning applies directly: a recorded observation cannot be securely classified when the necessary measurements were never collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-uk-project-condign-volume-1-uap-vol1-pgs14to34-ch2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufotransparency.com.</span></span></span>

For a County Down police report to become substantially stronger, investigators would need the original photograph or video; exact time and observing position; compass bearing and angle above the horizon; duration and movement; weather and cloud information; independent witnesses; and checks against aircraft, drone, astronomical and maritime activity. Radar or other sensor confirmation would be particularly valuable. The current Bangor, Cloghy, Downpatrick and Saintfield entries lack most or all of these elements.

## What the logs genuinely establish

The PSNI records have real historical value. They show that County Down residents continue to notice and report puzzling lights, and they preserve approximate locations and the language witnesses used before details disappear from public memory. They may also help identify clusters if several closely timed callers independently describe the same phenomenon.

Their evidential reach remains narrow, however. The records establish that calls were received; they do not independently establish the size, distance, behaviour or physical nature of the reported objects. The Bangor entry contains features compatible with an aircraft and possible phone-camera effects, but insufficient information for a conclusive identification. The Cloghy recollection is too vague for meaningful analysis, while the 2021 Downpatrick and Saintfield reports were not investigated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psni.police.uk/foi-disclosure-log/sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psni.police.uk">[Police Service of Northern Ireland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psni.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Police Service of Northern IrelandSightings &#124; PSNIJune 23, 2025 — Sightings &#124; PSNI Home / FOI Disclosure Log / Sightings Sightings June 2...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 23, 2025</span></span></span>

The fairest classification is therefore **unresolved because of missing data**, not “confirmed unexplained craft”. That wording matters. An unresolved report can remain unidentified simply because it was brief, delayed or poorly documented. County Down’s police logs preserve sincere and sometimes intriguing testimony, but on the publicly available evidence they do not demonstrate exotic aircraft, extraordinary technology or extraterrestrial visitation.

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
