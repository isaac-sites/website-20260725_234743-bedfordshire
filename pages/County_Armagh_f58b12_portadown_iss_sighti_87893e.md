## Introduction

The bright, silent light reported from Portadown on 10 May 2025 was almost certainly the International Space Station rather than an unknown aircraft. Three witnesses described a single white light moving swiftly from the south-west across the sky, without flashing lights or audible engine noise. NASA’s independently calculated viewing forecast placed the ISS on almost exactly that route over nearby Armagh at about the same time, reaching roughly the same height reported by the witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=189523" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No of observers: 3 Location: Portad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 Occurred: 2025-05-10 23:45 Local - Approximate Reported...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2025</span></span></span>

The recorded times are not identical: the witnesses gave an approximate time of 11.45 pm, whereas the predicted ISS pass began at about 11.35 pm. The reported observation also lasted only about a minute, compared with a predicted six-minute pass. Those differences prevent absolute proof, but they are modest beside the agreement in date, direction, altitude, appearance and movement. Within [County Armagh]({{ 'what-really-flew-over-county-armagh/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history, the case matters because it shows how an impressive sighting can be tested against information calculated before the report was submitted.

## What the witnesses reported

The sighting was submitted to the US-based National UFO Reporting Center, or NUFORC, shortly after it occurred. According to the report, three people were watching the night sky from a back garden in Portadown at approximately 11.45 pm on Saturday 10 May 2025. They had already watched Starlink satellites and had noticed a DHL aircraft passing overhead when another bright object appeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=189523" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No of observers: 3 Location: Portad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 Occurred: 2025-05-10 23:45 Local - Approximate Reported...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2025</span></span></span>

The observers said the light appeared to move faster than the aeroplane and seemed to be near the edge of the atmosphere. They also checked a flight-tracking service and found no corresponding aircraft. The object was therefore unidentified to them at the time, which is all that the label “UFO” strictly establishes.

There was no report of a solid outline, structured body, abrupt acceleration, manoeuvring or interaction with the aircraft. Nor does the public entry include a photograph, video, compass bearing or precisely timed sequence. The case rests on a short written account, but that account is unusually testable because it supplies a date, approximate time, direction of approach and estimated height.

## How the predicted ISS pass matched

NUFORC compared the description with NASA’s Spot the Station information for Armagh, the nearest listed forecast location. NASA’s prediction showed a visible ISS pass beginning at approximately 11.35 pm on 10 May. It was expected to remain visible for six minutes, first appearing about 10 degrees above the south-western horizon, rising to a maximum elevation of 40 degrees and disappearing about 11 degrees above the eastern horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=189523" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No of observers: 3 Location: Portad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 Occurred: 2025-05-10 23:45 Local - Approximate Reported...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2025</span></span></span>

That forecast matches the principal features of the Portadown report remarkably well.

**Direction:** The witnesses said the light came from the south-west. NASA also predicted that the ISS would appear in the south-west.

**Height:** The estimated elevation was 45 degrees. The predicted maximum was 40 degrees. An unaided estimate made in darkness can easily differ by five degrees, particularly when there is no fixed reference point in the sky.

**Appearance:** NASA describes the ISS as resembling a very bright star or an aeroplane moving across the sky, but without the flashing lights associated with an aircraft. That is essentially the appearance recorded at Portadown: one steady, brilliant white light with no coloured flashes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/spot-the-station/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spot The Station</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The space station appears similar to an airplane or a very bright star moving across the night sky, except it does not have flashing ligh...</span></span></span>

**Movement:** A visible ISS pass is smooth and continuous. It does not hover, turn or weave. The reported light likewise travelled steadily across the sky.

**Silence:** The ISS orbits hundreds of kilometres above Earth and cannot produce audible engine noise for an observer on the ground. Its silence is therefore expected, even when it appears visually striking. The witnesses’ ability to hear the DHL aircraft at the same time strengthens the contrast but does not make the unidentified light more mysterious.

No one of these characteristics would identify the ISS by itself. A distant aircraft, another satellite or even a bright object seen through thin cloud might share some of them. Taken together with a separately predicted pass on the same path, however, they form a strong cumulative match.

## Do the timing differences undermine the explanation?

The main reservation is the ten-minute difference between the approximate sighting time and NASA’s forecast. The report lists the event at 11.45 pm, while the ISS was predicted to appear at 11.35 pm. It also says the light was observed for about one minute rather than the forecast six minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=189523" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No of observers: 3 Location: Portad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 Occurred: 2025-05-10 23:45 Local - Approximate Reported...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2025</span></span></span>

These discrepancies should not be ignored, but neither is necessarily serious.

The event time was explicitly marked as approximate. The report was filed after the sighting rather than generated automatically by a camera or instrument, so it may reflect a later glance at a clock, a rounded estimate or imperfect recollection. A ten-minute error is plausible in an informal observation made while several objects were being watched.

Similarly, six minutes was the potential visibility window for an observer with a clear horizon and attention fixed on the correct part of the sky. The witnesses may not have noticed the ISS immediately after it rose in the south-west. Trees, buildings, haze or their attention to Starlink and the aircraft could have concealed the early portion. They may also have stopped following it before it reached the eastern horizon or entered Earth’s shadow.

Historical ISS predictions can shift slightly when orbital information is updated, although not normally enough to explain a ten-minute difference on their own. UK observing guides warn that pass times may vary somewhat by location and can change following orbital adjustments, but Portadown and Armagh are close enough that geography should account for only a small part of the discrepancy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://meteorwatch.org/iss-international-space-station-times-uk-may-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: meteorwatch.org">[meteorwatch.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">meteorwatch.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">iss international space station times uk may 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Space Station Times UK May 2025May 10, 2025 — International Space Station Times UK May 2025 Telescopes for Beginners – The...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 2025</span></span></span>

The most cautious judgement is therefore not that the match is mathematically exact. It is that an approximate human time conflicts slightly with a much stronger set of matching spatial and visual details. Unless reliable timestamped evidence emerges placing the light in the sky after the ISS had already passed, the timing issue is too weak to outweigh the identification.

## Why the ISS can look unlike an ordinary satellite

The ISS is technically a satellite, but it can appear far brighter than the small points of light people usually associate with satellites. Its large structure reflects sunlight while the observer below is already in darkness. Under favourable conditions it can become one of the brightest moving objects in the night sky and is plainly visible without a telescope.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/spot-the-station-frequently-asked-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spot the Station Frequently Asked Questions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">However, unlike the Moon, the space station is not bright enough to see during the day.... The Spot the Station mobile app is an officia...</span></span></span>

Its apparent behaviour can also be deceptive. Because there are few distance cues in a clear sky, witnesses cannot readily judge whether a light is a low aircraft, a high-altitude object or something in orbit. A bright ISS pass may consequently seem relatively close, unusually large or extraordinarily fast. The Portadown estimate that the object was near “the edge of the atmosphere” was not a measured distance; it was a visual impression.

A satellite may also appear to fade or vanish suddenly. This usually happens when it enters Earth’s shadow and stops reflecting direct sunlight towards the observer. NASA notes that ISS visibility depends on illumination by the Sun rather than moonlight, which is why it is most readily seen around dawn or dusk and can disappear even in an otherwise clear patch of sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/spot-the-station-frequently-asked-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spot the Station Frequently Asked Questions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">However, unlike the Moon, the space station is not bright enough to see during the day.... The Spot the Station mobile app is an officia...</span></span></span>

The witnesses had already seen Starlink objects that evening, but recognising one kind of satellite does not guarantee recognition of another. A Starlink train generally appears as several aligned points, especially soon after launch. The ISS presents a different spectacle: one exceptionally bright, steady point crossing a large portion of the sky.

## Why flight-tracking apps can mislead observers

The absence of the light from a flight-tracking app initially encouraged the idea that it was not an ordinary object. In this case that check was aimed at the wrong category. Services such as Flightradar24 display aircraft data; they are not maps of satellites or every object visible in the sky. The ISS would not be expected to appear as an aeroplane on such an app.

Even when the unknown light really is an aircraft, a blank map does not prove otherwise. Flightradar24 builds its display from sources including Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast signals, multilateration, radar information where available and airline schedule data. Coverage depends partly on whether suitable receivers can collect a particular aircraft’s transmissions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.fr24.com/support/solutions/articles/3000117422-how-does-flightradar24-work-" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.fr24.com">[Support]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.fr24.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Support How does Flightradar24 work?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flightradar24 Support centerSeptember 29, 2022 — The ADS-B, MLAT, and radar data are aggregated together with schedule and flight status...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 29, 2022</span></span></span>

Some do not carry compatible equipment, some are outside adequate receiver coverage, and some military or government flights are restricted from public display. Low-flying aircraft may also be difficult for ground receivers to detect because terrain and the curvature of the Earth interfere with line-of-sight radio reception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.fr24.com/support/solutions/articles/3000117423-why-are-some-aircraft-missing-on-the-map-" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.fr24.com">[Support]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.fr24.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Support Why are some aircraft missing on the map?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flightradar24 Support centerSeptember 29, 2022 — ## Why are some aircraft missing on the map?... They should be treated as one evidence source rather than a complete inventory of the sky. A useful sequence is to check aircraft traffic, satellite passes, bright planets, meteors and launch activity rather than concluding that “not on the app” means unexplainable.

## How strong is the identification?

The Portadown case is best classified as plausibly and strongly explained, not merely unresolved. Its evidential value lies less in the original mystery than in the quality of the later comparison.

The ISS prediction was independent of the witnesses’ interpretation. It was derived from orbital data and specified in advance when the station should appear, where it should emerge, how high it should rise and where it should leave view. The forecast did not simply offer a generic possibility that “a satellite may have been visible”. It reproduced the sighting’s most distinctive combination of features: a bright steady light, south-western approach, roughly 40-degree elevation, eastward movement and silence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=189523" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No of observers: 3 Location: Portad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 NUFORC UFO Sighting 189523 Occurred: 2025-05-10 23:45 Local - Approximate Reported...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2025</span></span></span>

There are still limits. The sighting lacks timestamped imagery, and the reported time and duration do not precisely match the prediction. It is therefore impossible to establish identification in the strict sense that might be possible from a video containing stars, landmarks and reliable metadata.

Yet alternative explanations fit less economically. An aircraft would need to mimic the predicted ISS track while showing no navigation flashes, producing no sound and failing to appear on the tracker. Another satellite is possible in the abstract, but the known passage of an unusually bright satellite on the reported route makes that additional hypothesis unnecessary. An exotic craft would explain no detail that the ISS does not already explain.

## Why the case matters in County Armagh

County Armagh’s reported UFO history consists mainly of brief observations with too little information for decisive investigation. The Portadown report is different because its time window and trajectory can be compared with an external astronomical prediction. It is one of the county’s clearest examples of an object becoming identified after the fact rather than remaining indefinitely mysterious.

The lesson is not that witnesses were careless. Their account was broadly accurate: something very bright and silent did cross from the south-west at approximately the reported height. The error lay in interpreting unfamiliar appearance as evidence of an unfamiliar object. Indeed, the directional and elevation details were good enough to support the eventual explanation.

Later reporting therefore weakened the UFO interpretation while preserving the credibility of the basic observation. There is no strong reason to doubt that the witnesses saw what they described. The best evidence indicates that what they saw was the International Space Station.

    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
