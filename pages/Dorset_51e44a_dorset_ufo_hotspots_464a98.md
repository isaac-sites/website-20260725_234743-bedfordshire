## Introduction

[Dorset]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-dorsets-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s apparent UFO hotspots depend heavily on which Dorset is being counted. Using the historic county, the clearest recent concentrations in published police records lie around Poole, Wareham and Purbeck, Weymouth, and a smaller western cluster including Beaminster and West Bay. Using the modern Dorset Police area, Bournemouth reports enter the total even though Bournemouth and Christchurch were historically in Hampshire. The result is a map that can make east Dorset look busier without showing any genuine increase inside historic Dorset.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Dorset_51e44a_dorset_ufo_hotspots_464a98-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Hotspots" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

There is another complication: police disclosure figures are not a complete census of sightings. They are keyword searches of incident logs, shaped by what callers said, what operators recorded and which search terms were chosen. A “hotspot” may therefore mark population, seafront visibility or reporting behaviour rather than unusual aerial activity. The most defensible conclusion is that Dorset has several recurring reporting zones, but the available figures are too small and inconsistent to establish a statistically meaningful UFO hotspot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

## Historic Dorset versus the modern police area

Historic Dorset and the territory currently served by Dorset Police are not interchangeable. The historic county includes Poole, Purbeck, Dorchester, Weymouth, Portland, Bridport and the western and northern Dorset countryside. Bournemouth and Christchurch, however, belonged historically to Hampshire. Dorset Council still warns researchers that most of its historic-map collection does not cover those two places for precisely that reason.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorsetcouncil.gov.uk/w/maps" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorsetcouncil.gov.uk">[Dorset Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorsetcouncil.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Council Historic maps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset CouncilHistoric maps - Dorset Council...</span></span></span>

Local-government reorganisation placed Bournemouth and Christchurch within modern Dorset’s administrative arrangements in 1974. Further structural changes created the present Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole unitary authority in 2019. Dorset Police now treats those three towns as one local policing area, alongside the force’s coverage of the rest of modern Dorset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive-catalogue.dorsetcouncil.gov.uk/records/RON/14/8/12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive-catalogue.dorsetcouncil.gov.uk">[archive-catalogue.dorsetcouncil.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive-catalogue.dorsetcouncil.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Local Government Reorganisation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New County of Dorset. That produces at least three legitimate but different maps:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Historic-county Dorset:** Bournemouth and Christchurch are excluded; Poole remains in Dorset.
* **Modern ceremonial or administrative Dorset:** Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole are included.
* **Dorset Police jurisdiction:** reports are grouped according to the force’s operational area, not historic county identity.

</div>

None of these frames is inherently wrong. The mistake is to quote a total produced under one geography as though it described another. A newspaper claim that “Dorset had 21 UFO reports”, for example, may actually mean that 21 qualifying records were found across the whole police jurisdiction, including places historically outside Dorset and incidents with no recorded location.

The problem becomes especially noticeable when comparing old cases with modern statistics. A sighting reported near Dorchester in 1967 belongs unambiguously to historic Dorset. A modern police call from Bournemouth belongs in a Dorset Police disclosure, but on the project’s historic-county map it sits in Hampshire. Combining the two without qualification creates a false continuity.

## What the police figures really show

The most useful public dataset is Dorset Police’s Freedom of Information disclosure covering April 2014 to April 2024. The force reported 21 sightings found by searching its incident system for terms including “UFO”, “UAP”, “spaceship” and “lights in the sky”. Five entries had no town or city stated. Of the located records, Bournemouth accounted for three, while the historic-Dorset locations comprised Poole, Wareham, Weymouth, Beaminster, Dorchester, West Bay and Thorncombe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

On a simple count, Poole appears first within historic Dorset, with four entries. Wareham, Weymouth and Beaminster each appear twice. Dorchester, West Bay and Thorncombe appear once each. Bournemouth has three reports, enough to alter any ranking based on the modern police area.

Those figures should not be treated as a measured rate of unexplained phenomena. The disclosure is a list of calls containing selected words, not the outcome of a dedicated investigation programme. Its entries range from vague statements about lights to an October 2023 Poole caller who reported approximately 18 “football size black triangles”. The summaries provide no accompanying photographs, flight checks, astronomical comparisons or final identifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

The search method also changes between disclosures. A request covering January 2019 to July 2023 searched for “UFO” and “lights in sky” and returned five records: two from Bournemouth and one each from Poole, West Bay and Weymouth. A broader ten-year search, using a larger vocabulary, produced many more results. The 2024-only search added terms such as “UAV”, “drones” and “orbs”, but Dorset Police said most matches concerned ordinary drone incidents, restricted airspace or requests for its drone unit; after review, only one Dorchester report remained in the published table.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/archive/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

This makes year-to-year totals unsafe to compare unless the keywords, databases and exclusion rules are identical. A rise may reflect a broader search rather than a real increase in reports. A fall may mean that different words were used or that callers contacted another organisation instead of the police.

## Poole and Purbeck form the strongest historic-Dorset cluster

Poole is the clearest modern concentration within historic Dorset, but even here “hotspot” is a cautious label. The ten-year police disclosure contains four Poole entries: a red flashing light in April 2018, photographed lights in November 2021, an “unidentified flying object” in March 2022 and the multiple black-triangle report of October 2023.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

The cluster is notable because Poole Harbour opens onto a broad southern horizon. Observers around the harbour, cliffs and beaches can see aircraft, vessels, navigation lights, stars and distant lights across water, often without nearby objects to provide scale. Such viewing conditions are good for noticing unusual-looking lights but poor for estimating their height, distance and speed. Four terse police entries therefore establish repeated reporting, not a common cause.

Poole also demonstrates why historic boundaries matter. It is now institutionally grouped with Bournemouth and Christchurch, yet it was already part of Dorset before the 1974 reorganisation. A “BCP hotspot” combines one historic-Dorset town with two historically Hampshire towns. For an operational police study that may be sensible; for a historic-county UFO map it conceals the boundary running through the urban area.

Wareham supplies a second concentration, with two reports only four days apart in September 2020. One caller said that UFOs were outside; another described glowing objects or faint lights in the sky. The timing suggests either repeated observation, repeated reporting or a shared local stimulus, but the summaries do not establish whether the calls came from the same witness, the same precise place or the same object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

Wareham is often used as the gateway label for Purbeck, so these reports can easily become “Purbeck sightings” in secondary retellings. That may be geographically reasonable, but it should not be stretched into evidence of a peninsula-wide flap. Two minimally documented calls are too little to show a persistent Purbeck hotspot, particularly without weather, aircraft, astronomical or event data.

## Weymouth and western Dorset show a coastal pattern

Weymouth appears twice in the ten-year police list. In February 2023 a caller reported three orange lights while outside for a cigarette; in March 2024 another caller simply reported lights in the sky. ## Why Bournemouth and Christchurch complicate totals

Bournemouth can appear to be one of Dorset’s leading UFO-reporting locations because it contributes three entries to the 2014–24 police disclosure. These include an April 2018 report of lights said not to be an aeroplane, a March 2019 claim that a spaceship was flying overhead and an October 2020 report of bangs, smoke and an orange light. Another police disclosure records a July 2020 caller who thought a UFO was visible over the sea south of Bournemouth Pier. Two officers near the clifftop reported seeing nothing similar, and the incident was logged with no further action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dorset.police.uk/foi-ai/dorset-police/disclosure-logs/2024-disclosures/ufo-sightings2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dorset.police.uk">[Dorset Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dorset.police.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dorset Police UFO Sightings &#124; Dorset Police</span></span></span>

That July 2020 entry is particularly useful because it shows the difference between an unexplained report and an unresolved object. The caller’s observation could not be confirmed by officers in the area. The record does not prove that nothing was present, but neither does it support the stronger claim that police witnessed or investigated a mysterious craft.

Historically, Bournemouth belonged to Hampshire. A historic report labelled “Bournemouth” may need a date and precise address before it can be placed confidently against a particular boundary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visionofbritain.org.uk/unit/10124906" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visionofbritain.org.uk">[Vision of Britain]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visionofbritain.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visionofbritain.org.uk.</span></span></span>

Christchurch creates the same conceptual problem even though it does not appear in the published ten-year sighting table. What matters is that any future Christchurch entry would increase a Dorset Police total while remaining outside historic Dorset.

For a clean county history, Bournemouth and Christchurch should therefore be presented as neighbouring Hampshire locations included in modern Dorset policing and administration. They remain relevant to shared media coverage, coastal viewing conditions and regional aviation, but should be marked separately when calculating historic-Dorset hotspots.

## Are these places genuine UFO hotspots?

The word “hotspot” can mean several different things. It may describe a place with the largest raw number of reports, a higher rate per resident, repeated sightings of the same phenomenon, a cluster within a short period, or an area containing unusually strong cases. Dorset’s available records do not measure all of these.

Poole leads the historic-county police list by raw count, but four reports across roughly a decade are not enough to establish an abnormal rate. Wareham’s two reports in four days resemble a small temporal cluster, yet their sparse descriptions prevent meaningful comparison. Weymouth has recurring reports across separate years, but neither released entry contains strong supporting evidence. Beaminster’s repeated light is potentially the most regular observation, although that regularity may favour an ordinary recurring source rather than something exceptional.

Several biases can create apparent concentrations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **Population:** More residents and visitors produce more potential observers.
* **Coastal visibility:** Open horizons expose more sky and distant lights but reduce reliable depth cues.
* **Night-time activity:** Resorts, harbours and entertainment areas keep more people outdoors after dark.
* **Reporting routes:** Some witnesses call police; others contact airports, newspapers, UFO organisations or nobody.
* **Place-label bias:** Reports may be filed under the caller’s town rather than the object’s apparent position.
* **Search-language bias:** “Lights in the sky” finds different incidents from a narrow search for “UFO”.
* **Boundary bias:** Adding Bournemouth changes both the total and the ranking without changing events inside historic Dorset.

</div>

The police data also contain no population adjustment. Poole or Bournemouth would be expected to produce more calls than a small inland village simply because more people live there and more visitors use their waterfronts. A proper hotspot analysis would calculate reports per head, retain precise coordinates and times, separate repeat callers, and compare sightings with aircraft, satellite, astronomical, weather and public-event records.

## A clearer way to map Dorset’s reports

The most reliable presentation is a layered map rather than a single headline total. Historic Dorset should form the main layer, with modern administrative and police boundaries shown separately. Each incident should be assigned at least two geographical fields: where the observer was located and where the object was reportedly seen. That distinction is essential for coastal and border cases.

Reports can then be grouped without exaggerating their quality:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--benefit" markdown="1">

* **Historic-Dorset reporting clusters:** Poole, Wareham/Purbeck, Weymouth and the western Dorset corridor.
* **Modern-area additions:** Bournemouth and any Christchurch reports.
* **Unlocated records:** retained in totals but excluded from hotspot mapping.
* **Border observations:** marked as uncertain when the apparent object may have been over Devon, Somerset, Hampshire or the sea.
* **Evidence strength:** distinguished between a brief call, multiple witnesses, photographs, official corroboration and cases with a plausible identification.

</div>

Under that approach, Poole is the leading recent reporting centre within historic Dorset, while Bournemouth becomes a significant modern-police-area addition rather than proof of an eastern historic-Dorset hotspot. Wareham and Beaminster are small clusters worth noting but not overstating. Weymouth and West Bay fit a broader coastal-reporting pattern in which open views and distant lights can produce both genuine puzzles and ordinary misidentifications.

The central lesson is not that Dorset has one hidden centre of extraordinary activity. It is that hotspot claims are highly sensitive to maps, databases and definitions. Until reports are consistently geocoded and investigated, Dorset’s “hotspots” are best understood as places where UFO claims have been recorded more than once—not places where unusual craft have been shown to occur more often.
