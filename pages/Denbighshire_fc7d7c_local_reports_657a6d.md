## Introduction

Llangernyw and Llanfair Talhaiarn are examples of how small rural communities became part of [Denbighshire]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-denbighshire/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history through reports of unusual lights, local investigations and claims about physical traces on the landscape. The evidence is not comparable with a formally investigated aviation incident or a radar-confirmed event: these stories are mainly built from witness accounts, newspaper coverage and later UFO research. Their importance lies in showing how local observations became part of a wider pattern of Welsh UFO folklore during the late twentieth century.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results/1990-01-01/1999-12-31?basicsearch=ufo&amp;county=denbighshire%2C+wales&amp;mostspecificlocation=denbighshire%2C+wales&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false&amp;somesearch=ufo&amp;sortorder=score" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Newspaper ArchiveResults for &#x27;ufo&#x27; &#124; Between 1st Jan 1990 and 31st Dec 1999 &#124; Denbighshire, Wales &#124; Location &#124; British Newspaper...</span></span></span>

Two reports stand out. Llangernyw became associated with accounts of strange lights and with local UFO enthusiast Margaret Fry, who collected reports from the area. ## Llangernyw lights and community reporting

Llangernyw’s place in Denbighshire UFO stories comes less from one single documented incident than from a cluster of local reports and the activity of residents who took an interest in unusual sightings. A 1998 report in the *Abergele & Pensarn Visitor* described Llangernyw resident Margaret Fry as someone who collected reports of strange lights and encouraged people to report unusual observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results/1990-01-01/1999-12-31?basicsearch=ufo&amp;country=wales&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false&amp;somesearch=ufo&amp;sortorder=score" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Newspaper ArchiveResults for &#x27;ufo&#x27; &#124; Between 1st Jan 1990 and 31st Dec 1999 &#124; British Newspaper Archive...</span></span></span>

This type of community reporting was important in the pre-social-media period. Local UFO researchers often relied on newspaper notices, letters and personal networks to gather accounts that might otherwise never have entered the public record. Rural areas such as Llangernyw offered dark skies and long sightlines, conditions that can make ordinary aerial objects appear more unusual because there are fewer nearby reference points for judging distance, speed and size.

A more dramatic account linked to Llangernyw appeared in UFO literature describing an April 1984 event in which local people reportedly saw a purple, oval-shaped object or mass descending towards the ground. The surviving references are largely through UFO publications and secondary accounts rather than an official investigation record, making it difficult to assess the original observation in detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/NewsClippings/UFO-Newsclipping-Service/UFO-Newsclipping-Service-1993-03-no-284.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Newsclipping Service 1993 03 no 284</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project AquariusUF.O. The main historical value of these reports is therefore not that they prove an unexplained object was present, but that they demonstrate how a rural community became part of a wider network of UFO reporting in north Wales. The absence of photographs, radar data or independent measurements means the reports remain unresolved rather than confirmed. This reflects a common pattern in historical UFO files: many reports contain sincere witness testimony but lack the information needed to distinguish unusual events from ordinary causes. The Ministry of Defence’s released UFO records similarly show that many reports consisted of descriptions of lights, shapes and movements that were later considered explainable by aircraft, satellites, planets or other conventional sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## The Voel Farm marks controversy

### Three circles that became a UFO story

The most distinctive Llanfair Talhaiarn case began in 1990 at Voel Farm, where reports described three large circular areas of burnt or damaged grass. The story was reported in the *North Wales Weekly News* under the headline “‘UFO’ circles in grass baffle the boffins”, with farmer Pat Coulson’s land becoming the focus of speculation about whether the marks represented a landing site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results/1990-01-01/1999-12-31?basicsearch=ufo&amp;county=denbighshire%2C+wales&amp;mostspecificlocation=denbighshire%2C+wales&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false&amp;somesearch=ufo&amp;sortorder=score" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Newspaper ArchiveResults for &#x27;ufo&#x27; &#124; Between 1st Jan 1990 and 31st Dec 1999 &#124; Denbighshire, Wales &#124; Location &#124; British Newspaper...</span></span></span>

The timing helped the story gain attention. The early 1990s were the peak period of public fascination with crop circles and mysterious ground markings in Britain. Reports of circles, rings and other patterns were receiving widespread media coverage, with some researchers proposing unusual natural [explanations]({{ 'explanations-209456/' | relative_url }}) while others suggested unknown forces or UFO activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/Archives/1990/07/24/Do-little-green-men-cause-those-circles/1542648792000/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Do &#x27;little green men&#x27; cause those circles?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Do &#x27;little green men&#x27; cause those circles? - UPI ArchivesJuly 24, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 24, 1990</span></span></span>

In Llanfair Talhaiarn, the key claim was not that anyone had witnessed a craft landing, but that the marks themselves appeared unusual. That distinction matters. A physical mark can feel more compelling than a brief light in the sky, but without controlled examination it remains difficult to establish what created it.

### What the evidence shows


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The strongest evidence for the Voel Farm story is the existence of the reported marks and the fact that they attracted local and newspaper interest. There is no publicly available scientific investigation showing that the circles were caused by a spacecraft or unknown technology. Nor is there a documented Ministry of Defence investigation linking the site to UFO activity.

</div>

Alternative explanations remain possible. Circular patches in grass can arise from a range of natural or human causes, including plant growth patterns, disease, animal activity, agricultural effects or deliberate creation. During the wider 1990 crop-circle debate, researchers considered explanations ranging from atmospheric effects to hoaxes, while later revelations about some crop circles being deliberately made changed public perceptions of many similar claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/Archives/1990/07/24/Do-little-green-men-cause-those-circles/1542648792000/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Do &#x27;little green men&#x27; cause those circles?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Do &#x27;little green men&#x27; cause those circles? - UPI ArchivesJuly 24, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 24, 1990</span></span></span>

The Voel Farm case therefore sits in an important middle category: it was a genuine local event that attracted attention, but the available evidence does not establish the cause. It remains a curiosity within Denbighshire UFO history rather than a verified landing claim.

## How local stories gained attention

The Llangernyw and Llanfair Talhaiarn reports show how UFO stories develop at a local level. A sighting or unexplained mark often begins with a small number of witnesses, then grows through newspapers, local investigators and wider UFO networks. The process can preserve valuable eyewitness accounts, but it can also add layers of interpretation as a story is repeated over time.

For Denbighshire, these reports are best understood alongside other county cases, including the better-known Denbigh sightings of 1979. Together they show a recurring theme: unusual lights and objects were reported across the county, but the strength of the evidence varied considerably. Some cases involved multiple witnesses; others depended mainly on interpretation after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>
