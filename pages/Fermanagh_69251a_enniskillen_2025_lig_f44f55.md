## Introduction

The 2025 Enniskillen lights are [Fermanagh]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-fermanaghs-skies/' | relative_url }})’s most detailed recent UFO claim, but the available evidence does not support the dramatic interpretation placed upon them. In late February, a local man described a bright white light moving rapidly over Enniskillen, apparently stopping, climbing and continuing sideways before a green light appeared nearby. He said he had recorded the event, reviewed the footage frame by frame and checked flight routes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fermanaghherald.com/2025/02/ufo-sighting-in-fermanagh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fermanaghherald.com">[The Fermanagh Herald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fermanaghherald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Fermanagh Herald UFO sighting in Fermanagh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fermanagh HeraldUFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh HeraldFebruary 28, 2025 — UFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh Herald...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 28, 2025</span></span></span>

Those details make the report more substantial than a fleeting “strange light” anecdote. ## What the witness said he saw

The account was published by the *Fermanagh Herald* on 28 February 2025. The newspaper said the observation had occurred over Enniskillen during the previous week, but did not give an exact date, clock time, duration, viewing direction or street-level location. These omissions make it difficult to reconstruct the sky as it appeared to the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fermanaghherald.com/2025/02/ufo-sighting-in-fermanagh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fermanaghherald.com">[The Fermanagh Herald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fermanaghherald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Fermanagh Herald UFO sighting in Fermanagh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fermanagh HeraldUFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh HeraldFebruary 28, 2025 — UFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh Herald...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 28, 2025</span></span></span>

His military background was included in the article, as was an earlier experience in which he believed he had seen a large black triangle while travelling to Wales during the 1980s. That history may explain why he took the Enniskillen observation seriously, but it does not independently verify either event. Experience can help a witness recognise familiar aircraft, yet it cannot supply missing measurements such as distance, altitude or true speed.

The most important weakness lies in phrases such as “across the sky” and “cover ground”. A light’s apparent movement across a person’s field of view does not reveal how much physical ground it covered unless its distance is known. A nearby insect, bird or small drone can cross a large visual angle quickly, while a distant aircraft can appear nearly stationary when approaching head-on. The report gives no reliable range estimate from which the object’s speed could be calculated.

## What evidence is publicly available?

The public evidence appears to consist of one newspaper interview. The article does not display the video said to have been recorded, provide still frames from it or identify an investigator who received the original file. It also contains no photograph of the viewing scene, map of the reported path, technical metadata or testimony from anyone who watched the event alongside the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fermanaghherald.com/2025/02/ufo-sighting-in-fermanagh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fermanaghherald.com">[The Fermanagh Herald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fermanaghherald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Fermanagh Herald UFO sighting in Fermanagh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fermanagh HeraldUFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh HeraldFebruary 28, 2025 — UFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh Herald...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 28, 2025</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because a video can be examined properly only when its context is preserved. Investigators would need the unedited original rather than a compressed social-media copy, together with the recording time, device model, lens setting and any zoom or stabilisation information. Fixed buildings, rooflines or stars could then be used to test whether the light itself changed direction or whether the camera moved.

The reported disappearance of the green light behind a building could be particularly useful. If the precise observation point and building were known, they might define a line of sight and help establish the light’s direction. Without those details, the building functions only as a narrative landmark: it shows that the light was visually obscured, but not how distant, high or fast it was.

The account also lacks independent corroboration. The witness explicitly wondered whether anyone else had seen the lights, but the published article does not name a second observer. No corresponding statement from air traffic control, the Police Service of Northern Ireland, an airport operator or a meteorological body is presented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fermanaghherald.com/2025/02/ufo-sighting-in-fermanagh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fermanaghherald.com">[The Fermanagh Herald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fermanaghherald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Fermanagh Herald UFO sighting in Fermanagh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fermanagh HeraldUFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh HeraldFebruary 28, 2025 — UFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh Herald...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 28, 2025</span></span></span>

This does not mean that the witness invented the experience. It means that the most unusual parts of the description — instantaneous-looking acceleration, a stop in mid-flight and a sudden vertical movement — depend on one person’s visual interpretation and an unavailable recording. In evidence terms, the claim is detailed but not independently testable.

## Does checking a flight-tracking app rule out aircraft?

The witness said that he checked flight routes and found no aircraft travelling that way at the relevant time. That was a sensible step, but a negative result from a public tracking service cannot by itself exclude every aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fermanaghherald.com/2025/02/ufo-sighting-in-fermanagh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fermanaghherald.com">[The Fermanagh Herald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fermanaghherald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Fermanagh Herald UFO sighting in Fermanagh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fermanagh HeraldUFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh HeraldFebruary 28, 2025 — UFO sighting in Fermanagh - The Fermanagh Herald...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 28, 2025</span></span></span>

Services such as Flightradar24 rely chiefly on signals transmitted by aircraft and received by a network of ground stations or satellites. Aircraft without a compatible Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast transponder may instead be located through multilateration, which requires several receivers to detect the same signal. Coverage can become less complete at lower altitude or where terrain obstructs reception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightradar24.com/about_mobile.php?device=ios" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightradar24.com">[Flightradar24]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightradar24.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Satellites are collecting the ADS-B signals from aircraft and transmitting them to the Flightradar24 network.... Flightradar24 itself notes that an aircraft may not appear where ADS-B or multilateration coverage is limited. General aviation, military and other specialised flights are not necessarily represented as completely as scheduled airline traffic. A meaningful flight check would require, at minimum, the exact date and time, the observer’s position, viewing direction and the tracking service examined. Ideally, it would compare several data sources and retain screenshots or downloaded records. None of that material accompanies the published Enniskillen account.

There is also a difference between checking scheduled routes and checking actual traffic. An aircraft need not follow a regular airline corridor: police, medical, training, private, survey and military flights can move differently. Consequently, “no plane on the app” reduces the likelihood of an ordinary tracked airliner, but does not establish an empty sky.

## Aircraft, drones and the green light

The presence of green and white light is not inherently exotic. Conventional aircraft use red, green and white navigation lights, with green marking the starboard, or right-hand, side and white generally visible from behind. Bright landing or anti-collision lights can dominate at some angles, while another navigation light appears only after the aircraft turns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caa.co.uk/publication/download/13280" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caa.co.uk">[Civil Aviation Authority]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caa.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Civil Aviation AuthorityPaper 2006/04 Part 1July 27, 2006 — &#x27;Aircraft have red, green and white navigation lights, the same colour code a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2006</span></span></span>

This creates several ways in which one aircraft might appear to produce changing colours or apparently separate lights. A turn can reveal the green starboard lamp after a powerful forward-facing white light has been most prominent. Buildings or rooflines may temporarily conceal one lamp while leaving another visible. Atmospheric haze, cloud and automatic camera exposure can further alter the apparent colour and size.

Two aircraft at different distances are another possibility. With no visible bodies or depth cues, separate points of light can appear close together even when they are miles apart. Conversely, lights mounted on one aircraft may seem detached when the dark airframe between them is invisible.

A drone cannot be dismissed solely by comparing the observation with a quoted maximum speed. The witness’s estimate depends on an unknown distance, and therefore does not establish a physical velocity. A nearby drone crossing the line of sight can seem much faster than a distant one; a sudden turn towards or away from the observer may look like a stop, jump or vertical climb.

## Could the camera have created the sharp movements?

Slow-motion review sounds rigorous, but it does not automatically make a phone recording an accurate record of motion. Frame-by-frame playback can expose movement; it can also magnify hand shake, digital stabilisation corrections, autofocus changes and compression artefacts.

A distant point light is an especially difficult target. It may occupy only a few sensor pixels, with no visible shape from which orientation can be judged. When exposure is set for darkness, a bright light can become saturated, meaning that it spreads into neighbouring pixels and appears larger than it really is. NASA’s guide to smartphone astrophotography explains that bright astronomical subjects can exceed a sensor’s recording capacity unless exposure is controlled, erasing detail rather than revealing it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/solar/2023/09/a/Astrophotography_Guide.pdf?emrc=69bb1e2623720" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nasa.gov.</span></span></span>

Several common effects could imitate aspects of the Enniskillen description:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Camera movement:** A small hand movement can make a distant light sweep rapidly across the frame. Without fixed stars or buildings visible throughout, the object’s movement cannot easily be separated from the phone’s.
* **Digital stabilisation:** Software may shift or crop successive frames to counter hand shake. Against a dark, featureless background, those corrections can make a light appear to jump or change course.
* **Autofocus and exposure changes:** A point light can expand, contract, brighten, fade or change colour as the camera searches for focus and alters its sensitivity.
* **Loss behind foreground objects:** A moving phone or changing viewpoint can make a distant light disappear behind a roofline, even when its path is steady.
* **Perspective during a turn:** An ordinary aircraft changing heading can appear to slow or stop because its sideways motion decreases as it turns towards the observer.

</div>

None of these effects proves that the reported manoeuvres were illusory. They demonstrate why the original file and a stable reference frame are essential before claims of extraordinary acceleration can be accepted.

## What would a proper investigation need?

The case could be strengthened or weakened substantially if the underlying material were released. The most useful evidence would be the original, unedited video with metadata intact. Investigators could compare frame times, camera movement, foreground edges and the light’s path rather than relying on verbal descriptions of slow-motion playback.

A basic reconstruction would also require:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. The exact date, start time and duration.
2. The witness’s viewing position and direction.
3. Identification of the building that obscured the green light.
4. The phone model, zoom level and whether stabilisation was active.
5. Complete flight-tracking searches covering nearby airports and low-altitude traffic.
6. Local weather observations, including cloud base, visibility, wind and precipitation.
7. Statements or recordings from any independent witnesses.
8. Checks for astronomical objects, satellites, meteors and local drone activity.

</div>

Radar evidence would be valuable but should not be treated as a simple pass-or-fail test. A small drone, bird or low-flying object might not produce a useful radar return, while the absence of a public radar record does not mean that no official data existed. The relevant question would be whether a trace corresponded in time and direction with the visual observation.

The available material has not reached that standard. There is no published chain of custody for the recording, no documented analysis by an aviation or imaging specialist and no indication that the sighting was formally investigated by a public authority.

## Does the claim withstand scrutiny?

The report survives scrutiny only in a limited sense: an Enniskillen resident gave a comparatively detailed and apparently sincere description of lights he could not identify.
