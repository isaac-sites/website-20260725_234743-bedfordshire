## Introduction

Reports of unusual night lights around Arbroath and Montrose are among the clearest examples of how ordinary events can become part of [Angus]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-angus-ufo/' | relative_url }}) UFO folklore. The best-documented cases involve bright violet, blue or purple-looking lights seen over the coast and nearby towns, with witnesses describing objects that appeared to float, move or shine through cloud. These reports are interesting not because they prove an extraordinary cause, but because they show how unfamiliar technology, atmospheric conditions and limited night-time perception can combine to create convincing “unknown” sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecourier.co.uk/fp/news/angus-mearns/2535146/angus-residents-baffled-by-patches-of-violet-light-in-night-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecourier.co.uk">[The Courier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecourier.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Courier Angus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CourierAngus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky - The CourierSeptember 15, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2021</span></span></span>

## The reported violet light events

The best-known Arbroath and Montrose night-light episode occurred in September 2021, when residents reported seeing patches of violet or blue-purple light in the sky. Witnesses described two light areas appearing to move slowly, disappear and reappear, and seem to sit within or behind cloud. Some observers initially wondered whether the lights could be connected to unusual aircraft activity or something more mysterious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecourier.co.uk/fp/news/angus-mearns/2535146/angus-residents-baffled-by-patches-of-violet-light-in-night-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecourier.co.uk">[The Courier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecourier.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Courier Angus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CourierAngus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky - The CourierSeptember 15, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2021</span></span></span>

The reports attracted attention because the lights did not resemble normal street lighting or familiar aircraft navigation lights. Their colour, apparent movement and interaction with cloud created an unusual visual effect. One St Vigeans witness near Arbroath described seeing what appeared to be two rounded patches of violet light and briefly considered whether it could involve a UFO or experimental equipment. However, the witness also described the event as curious rather than frightening, which is typical of many local night-sky reports where uncertainty rather than fear drives public interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecourier.co.uk/fp/news/angus-mearns/2535146/angus-residents-baffled-by-patches-of-violet-light-in-night-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecourier.co.uk">[The Courier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecourier.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Courier Angus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CourierAngus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky - The CourierSeptember 15, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2021</span></span></span>

The explanation emerged quickly. Network Rail confirmed that the lights came from its New Measurement Train, a specialist inspection train fitted with optical equipment used to monitor railway infrastructure. When the train’s lights interacted with low cloud, the illumination could appear to be suspended above the ground rather than clearly connected to a railway vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecourier.co.uk/fp/news/angus-mearns/2535146/angus-residents-baffled-by-patches-of-violet-light-in-night-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecourier.co.uk">[The Courier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecourier.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Courier Angus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CourierAngus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky - The CourierSeptember 15, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2021</span></span></span>

A similar event had already occurred along the Angus coast. In an earlier report, blue “saucer-like” lights were seen from locations including Arbroath and Montrose, with observers describing an eerie glow in the evening sky. The cause was again the New Measurement Train travelling along the railway line, with its inspection lights reflecting through cloud and creating a distant blue glow.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecourier.co.uk/fp/business-environment/transport/590925/video-did-you-see-this-freaky-blue-saucer-ignite-the-sky-in-tayside-this-is-what-caused-it/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecourier.co.uk">[The Courier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecourier.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CourierVIDEO: Did you see this &#x27;freaky&#x27; blue saucer ignite the sky in Tayside? These incidents became notable because the explanation was not obvious from the viewpoint of someone standing several miles away. The source was a railway vehicle on the ground, but the visual effect placed the light apparently in the sky. This is a useful reminder that the appearance of a light source does not always reveal its actual location.

## Why these lights looked unusual

Night-time light reports are especially vulnerable to misinterpretation because witnesses often lack depth, distance and scale information. A bright light against a dark sky provides few clues about whether it is close or far away, above the ground or reflected from clouds.

In the Arbroath and Montrose cases, several factors made the lights appear more mysterious:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Low cloud:** Cloud can scatter artificial light and make a ground-based source appear elevated or spread across the sky.
* **Unusual colour:** Blue and violet illumination is less common in everyday surroundings, making it more likely to attract attention.
* **Movement:** A moving source viewed through changing cloud can appear to vanish and return.
* **Distance from the source:** Observers along the coast were not necessarily able to see the railway equipment producing the effect.

</div>

The New Measurement Train is designed for railway safety rather than public display. It uses scanners, cameras and other monitoring systems to assess track and infrastructure conditions, travelling at speeds of up to 125 mph while collecting information about the railway network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.networkrail.co.uk/our-work/looking-after-the-railway/our-fleet-machines-and-vehicles/new-measurement-train-nmt/?trk=public_post_comment-text" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: networkrail.co.uk">[Network Rail]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">networkrail.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Network Rail New Measurement Train (NMT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Network RailNew Measurement Train (NMT) - Network Rail...</span></span></span> Its specialist equipment means it can produce effects unfamiliar to people who happen to see it operating at night.

This pattern is not unique to Angus. UK UFO records held by the National Archives contain many examples where initially unidentified lights were later associated with aircraft, satellites, balloons or other ordinary causes. The value of these records is not that every report is “explained away”, but that investigation often changes how a sighting should be understood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## How light reports are assessed

A night-light report is usually strongest when several independent types of evidence support the original observation. Investigators look at where and when the event occurred, whether other witnesses saw it, whether aircraft or other known objects were nearby, and whether environmental conditions could explain the appearance.

For Arbroath and Montrose, the strongest evidence came from matching the timing and location of witness reports with a known activity: the operation of Network Rail’s inspection train. The explanation did not depend on dismissing witnesses; rather, it accounted for why sincere observers saw something unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecourier.co.uk/fp/news/angus-mearns/2535146/angus-residents-baffled-by-patches-of-violet-light-in-night-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecourier.co.uk">[The Courier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecourier.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Courier Angus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CourierAngus residents baffled by &#x27;patches of violet light&#x27; in night sky - The CourierSeptember 15, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2021</span></span></span>

A useful distinction in UFO reporting is the difference between:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Unidentified at the time:** a person sees something they cannot immediately recognise.
* **Unresolved:** no convincing explanation has been found after investigation.
* **Explained:** later evidence identifies a likely cause.
