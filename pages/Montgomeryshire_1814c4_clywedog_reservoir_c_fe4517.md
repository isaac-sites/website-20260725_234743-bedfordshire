## Introduction

The alleged Clywedog Reservoir encounter of 22 October 1971 is [Montgomeryshire]({{ 'what-really-flew-over-montgomeryshire/' | relative_url }})’s most dramatic UFO story, but not its strongest case. According to an account first investigated and published more than 53 years later, a night-shift worker driving beside the reservoir saw a flaming disc cross low over the fields, experienced a stalled car, and then watched a dark, domed object hover above the water before accelerating away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Montgomeryshire_1814c4_clywedog_reservoir_c_fe4517-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Clywedog Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

What makes the story memorable is also what makes it difficult to assess: it combines a close-range object, apparent mechanical interference, a possible impact in water and a prolonged second observation. Yet every important detail now depends on one elderly witness’s retrospective testimony. No photograph, physical trace, named contemporary witness, confirmed official file or surviving original report has been produced. Later research has added narrative detail, but it has not supplied independent evidence—and one check on the remembered conditions raises a significant inconsistency.

## The reported roadside and reservoir encounter

The witness, identified only by the pseudonym “Howard”, was reportedly 28 and living near Staylittle on the north-eastern edge of Hafren Forest. At about 9pm on Friday 22 October 1971, he set out in an Austin 1100 for his night shift in Newtown. His normal route followed the B4518 past Clywedog Reservoir, which had been created only a few years earlier by dams built across the upper Clywedog valley.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

About five minutes into the journey, the published account says, Howard heard a loud “whoosh” from his right as his engine stopped. He then saw what appeared to be a disc completely surrounded by flames and throwing off sparks. He estimated that it was roughly 25 to 30 feet across, perhaps 100 yards from the road and only a few hundred feet above the ground. It followed a shallow downward path towards a hill concealing the main body of the reservoir and remained visible for more than 30 seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

Howard reportedly believed that the object would strike the hillside, although it narrowly cleared the summit and vanished beyond it. When he opened his bonnet, he found a spark-plug lead hanging loose. He reattached it, restarted the car and drove towards a position overlooking the water. This detail is important because the later investigator treated the engine failure as possible interference from the passing object. The account itself, however, supplies a direct mechanical reason for the stoppage: a disconnected ignition lead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

From the far side of the brow, Howard said he saw a second scene. A dark, gunmetal-grey disc appeared to be hovering above the middle of the reservoir. Only its upper half was initially visible. He described a central dome, a row of rectangular window-like features and a steady red light on the rim. The body seemed to rotate slowly while remaining otherwise motionless.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

After approximately 15 minutes, the object allegedly rose vertically to several hundred feet. Howard then saw what he interpreted as a second dome beneath it. It paused, moved towards the dam and suddenly departed south-west so rapidly that it disappeared in less than a second. He later concluded that the object was under intelligent control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

The witness’s own interpretation became more elaborate with time. He suggested that the burning object had crashed into the reservoir and that the hovering disc was a separate craft arriving to recover its occupants or technology. The investigator also proposed an alternative in which a single craft briefly entered the water to extinguish its flames before re-emerging. Neither version comes from an observed impact: Howard reportedly saw no splash, debris, fire on the water or wreckage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

## What evidence survives from the original claim?

The case rests chiefly on the testimony collected by Birmingham UFO Group investigator Dave Hodrien after Howard’s son made contact through Facebook in early 2025. The resulting report was released in July and republished by Swansea UFO Network in September 2025. Pseudonyms were used, so the public cannot independently check the witness’s identity, employment history or exact residence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

The surviving support can be divided into three limited strands.

**Howard’s retrospective account.** The investigator conducted several telephone conversations and regarded the witness as sincere, noting that he answered questions promptly and retained detailed memories. Sincerity matters, but it does not establish factual accuracy. The interviews were conducted more than half a century after the event, and no recording or statement made in 1971 has been published for comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

**The son’s recollection.** Howard’s son remembered hearing the story while growing up and vaguely recalled paperwork containing drawings. This supports the possibility that the story existed within the family well before 2025. It does not independently confirm what was seen, and the son apparently did not witness the incident or identify the paperwork conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

**Claims of contemporary reporting.** Howard said he telephoned the British UFO Research Association, or BUFORA, during the following weekend and returned a sighting form containing a chart. ## Where the account is most vulnerable

The greatest problem is not that the story is inherently impossible. It is that its precision was reconstructed after an exceptionally long delay without a surviving baseline document.

### The remembered moonlight does not match the date

Howard reportedly described an exceptionally bright Moon that illuminated the reservoir so clearly that he could see vegetation on the far bank. Astronomical calendars place 22 October 1971 only a few days after new Moon, with a thin waxing crescent rather than a bright or nearly full Moon. The full Moon that month had occurred on 2 October.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.calendarr.com/united-kingdom/lunar-calendar-1971/print/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: calendarr.com">[Calendarr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">calendarr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October... 18 Image: Waning Crescent &#124; 19 Image: New Moon New Moon &#124; 20 Image: Waxing Crescent &#124; 21 Image: Waxing Crescent &#124; 22 Image: W...</span></span></span>

This discrepancy matters because moonlight is central to the claimed viewing conditions. The witness said it enabled him to discern a dark object over the water, rectangular “windows”, slow rotation and details of the far shoreline. A low, slender crescent could not provide the illumination normally implied by that description, particularly at about 9pm in late October.

Several explanations remain possible. The date may be wrong; artificial light, twilight or reflected skyglow may have been remembered as moonlight; or the degree of visibility may have grown in recollection. None disproves that something unusual was seen. It does weaken confidence in the exact chronology and in fine visual details reconstructed decades later.

### Estimated size and distance were not independently measured

Descriptions such as “25 to 30 feet across”, “100 yards away” and “400 to 500 feet high” sound precise, but no angular measurements, fixed comparison points or original diagram survive. At night, especially over dark fields and open water, an observer cannot reliably determine an unfamiliar object’s true size without knowing its distance. A small nearby light and a much larger distant one can occupy the same apparent angle.

The second object’s position over the deepest part of the reservoir is similarly interpretive. From a roadside viewpoint, a light or silhouette seen against water and hills may appear to be directly above a particular point even when its actual position is nearer or farther away.

### The account joins two observations into one incident

The burning object disappeared behind a hill. Howard then repaired his car, drove on and stopped at another location before seeing the domed form. He did not continuously observe an object entering the reservoir and emerging from it. The conclusion that the two appearances involved the same craft—or two cooperating craft—is therefore an inference.

This gap allows several less dramatic combinations: a fireball followed by an unrelated aircraft light; burning debris followed by a reflection or misperceived distant object; or one unusual stimulus whose remembered stages became linked over time. The more elaborate “rescue craft” scenario adds actions and motives that were never observed directly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

## Meteor, mechanical fault and memory-based explanations

A meteor or bright fireball is the most obvious explanation for the first phase because it naturally accounts for flames, sparks, rapid movement and an apparent descent towards the horizon. Fireballs can fragment, produce sound and look much closer than they are. Scientifically reconstructed meteorite-dropping events normally occur tens of kilometres above the ground and travel at kilometres per second, although shallow “Earth-grazing” meteors can remain visible much longer than ordinary streaks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12126" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv The Winchcombe Fireball -- that Lucky Survivor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">On February 28, 2021, a fireball dropped $\sim0.6$ kg of recovered CM2 carbonaceous chondrite meteorites in South-West England near the t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 28, 2021</span></span></span>

Conversely, Howard’s estimate of roughly 100mph is incompatible with a luminous meteor if interpreted literally; it is more likely an impression derived from an assumed low altitude and distance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sufon.co.uk/post/clwedog-reservoir-powys-wales-domed-disc-crash-22nd-october-1971" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sufon.co.uk">[Swansea Ufo Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sufon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">clwedog reservoir powys wales domed disc crash 22nd october 1971</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swansea Ufo NetworkClwedog Reservoir, Powys, Wales – Domed Disc Crash-22nd October 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1971</span></span></span>

A meteor explanation is not complete. It does not account for a structured, hovering, rotating disc observed for 15 minutes. But the strength of that second description depends heavily on the disputed lighting conditions and on estimates made without optical aids. A distant helicopter or aircraft seen nearly head-on can appear stationary for a time, while navigation lights, haze, reflections and movement of the observer can suggest structure or rotation. The reported near-instantaneous departure would be extraordinary if accurately perceived, but it was not timed, photographed or witnessed from another location.

The car failure is less mysterious than it first appears. Howard reportedly found and corrected a loose spark-plug connection before the engine restarted. The case report speculates that air disturbance from a meteor might have dislodged it, but presents no mechanical evidence for that mechanism. Repeated retelling can preserve the central conviction that an extraordinary event occurred while altering dates, durations, lighting, distances and the relationship between separate impressions. The incorrect lunar description is exactly the kind of externally checkable detail that makes a contemporary report important. Without the alleged BUFORA form, researchers cannot determine whether “Howard” described the same sequence, date and visual features in 1971.

## Did later reporting strengthen the case?

Later reporting preserved a previously obscure Montgomeryshire story and made its claims testable. That has historical value. The published route, estimated times, vehicle details and family recollection provide leads that could still be checked against archives. The discovery of contemporary *Shropshire Star* UFO coverage also shows that unusual lights were being discussed in the wider region that week.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/search/results/1971-01-01/1971-12-31?basicsearch=ufo&amp;retrievecountrycounts=false" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk">[British Newspaper Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Newspaper ArchiveResults for &#x27;ufo&#x27; &#124; Between 1st Jan 1971 and 31st Dec 1971 &#124; British Newspaper ArchiveResults for &#x27;ufo&#x27; &#124; Betwee...</span></span></span>

On balance, however, publication has weakened rather than strengthened the case as evidence of an extraordinary craft. It reveals that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the first public, detailed account appeared more than five decades after the event;
* the witness remains anonymous;
* the alleged BUFORA form and newspaper article have not been matched to the incident;
* no independent observer or official record confirms an object at the reservoir;
* the car had an identifiable ignition fault;
* no impact, splash or wreckage was actually observed;
* modern illustrations are reconstructions rather than evidence;
* and the reported bright moonlight conflicts with the lunar phase on the stated date.

</div>

The fairest classification is therefore **unresolved testimony with weak evidential support**, not a confirmed UFO crash or water-emergence incident. A real observation may lie behind the account, and the burning first object could plausibly have been an impressive natural or aviation-related event. The later claim of a rotating domed craft cannot be explained conclusively from the surviving material, but neither can it be separated from long-term memory, mistaken distance and the loss of contemporary documentation.

Within Montgomeryshire’s UFO history, Clywedog matters less as proof of an extraordinary vehicle than as an example of how a vivid local encounter can become famous only after the evidence needed to evaluate it has largely disappeared.
